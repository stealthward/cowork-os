## The One Big Idea

The majority of real estate transactions come from someone who already knows the agent. So the entire game is to be the first agent someone thinks of when they, or someone they know, is ready to buy or sell. Everything in the book serves that single thesis.

## Promise of the Book

The reader walks away able to build a recognizable brand, grow an audience of the right people, and create content that keeps them top of mind, without a marketing team, big budget, or hours on social media. The payoff word is **clarity**.

## Reader (Hero)

Primary: any licensed real estate agent. Secondary emphasis: top producers who value market share, prestige, and reputation. Full avatar in 02-audience-positioning.md. In StoryBrand terms the reader is the hero; Brett (author) is the guide.

## Locked Terminology & Definitions

Use these consistently everywhere. If a definition changes, change it here first, then propagate.

- **Brand** = what people associate with you (a perception), not a logo.
- **Branding** = the process of shaping that perception.
- **Good content** = content that is interesting and relevant to the people who see it.
- **Audience** = the people who see your content.
- **The Three Spheres** = the audience-segmentation model:
  - **Sphere of Influence (SOI)** = people who think of you first for real estate, would hire you, and would refer others.
  - **Sphere of Recognition (SOR)** = people who know you by face or name but don't think of you first ("the growth zone").
  - **Sphere of Proximity (SOP)** = people in your market who don't know you yet ("the expansion zone").
- **The binary strategy** = every piece of content either Protects your SOI or Expands (grows) your SOI.
- **The Realtor Client Journey** = one path every client walks, buyer or seller: Awareness (life changes, by choice or by circumstance, and their home no longer fits), Consideration, Decision, Retention, Referrals. Content changes by stage AND sphere.
- **Lead** = has a problem you can solve and has shown interest in solving it (Consideration). **Prospect** = wants you to solve it and meets your criteria (Decision). **Client** = closed with you (Retention).
- **Qualified prospect** = ready (a real timeline), willing (motivated), able (pre-approved buyer or seller with equity), and a fit (your ideal client and market).

## Voice Calls (decisions made, apply everywhere)

- No em dashes, anywhere. Use commas, colons, or periods.
- Paragraphs of 3 sentences or fewer in most instances.
- ~6th-grade reading level. Short, punchy, declarative.

## Book Architecture (approved 2026-10-26)

Full map: 2026-10-26-reach-book-map.md.

- **Five parts:** I. Start Here (Ch 1 to 2), II. Get Known (Ch 3 to 5), III. Get Clients (Ch 6 to 7), IV. Get It Done (Ch 8 to 10), V. Get Started (Ch 11).
- **Sequence rule:** each part assumes what the last one gave the reader. Each chapter closes with "Next Up," which names the limit the reader just hit and the next chapter as the fix.
- **Chapter template:**
  1. Title and a "How to..." subtitle.
  2. Brett's own epigraph.
  3. Client-story cold open at a moment of struggle.
  4. "This chapter covers..." scope line.
  5. One framework, numbered first, then expanded.
  6. Signposts: Bottom line, Action step, Pro Tip (how we do it; teaches, never pitches).
  7. Objections.
  8. "How you know it's working" (sourced benchmarks only).
  9. Action items checklist.
  10. Next Up.
- **Book devices:**
  - A recurring cast of three or four agent clients at different stages (Brett picks them).
  - The flywheel diagram at each part opener with "you are here."
  - A preview opens each part and a recap closes it.
  - A one-page whole-book recap in Ch 11.
  - Visual-heavy pages.
  - Back matter is a light resource list, no pitch.
- **Weight:** Parts III and IV carry most of the book. Ch 8 to 10 grow to be among the longest chapters.
