## Core idea

1. **Be the first agent someone thinks of when they, *or someone they know*, is ready to buy or sell.**
2. **Build an audience with as many of these people as possible.**

## The Problem (raw)

Selling real estate isn't *easy*, but it is *simple*. And that's a good thing. As someone with a history of complicating things, I crave simplicity, especially in business. Simple is much easier to scale. Find out what works, and just do more of it.

**Brand Marketing = the methods you use to identify, attract, convert, and retain clients.**
- **Identify** your ideal client with clear characteristics
- **Attract** them with content that earns attention
- **Convert** with timely tools and offers
- **Retain** with consistency, proof, and referrals

Your definition of an ideal client will change over time, just like you do. The process of defining them stays the same, and it begins with building your personal profile and your ideal client profile.

## Audience (raw)

Your audience is simply "the people who see your content." But having an audience is useless if it's not filled with the right people. To make more money, the people in your audience need to: (1) buy and sell homes in your market, or know people who do; (2) think of you first when they, or someone they know, is ready to buy or sell.

### Ideal-client interest examples (with sample titles + content)

- **Family-friendly restaurants** — "Looking for a restaurant your kids will actually love?" Spotlight series: favorite dishes, kid-friendly features, quick owner/family interviews.
- **Date-night options** — "When was the last time you had a real date night?" Best-of reels: ambiance, signature dishes, couples' takes.
- **Local parks** — "This might be the best-kept secret park in town." Walkthroughs: playgrounds, trails, amenities, parent soundbites.
- **Youth sports** — "Need a place where your kids burn energy and build confidence?" League features: coach/player interviews, practice highlights, signup.
- **Fitness & wellness** — "Can you fit a full workout into your lunch break?" Class demos with instructors, testimonials from busy parents.
- **Community events** — "Here's how to make the most of this weekend's big event." On-location with organizers: history, insider tips.
- **Nearby schools** — "Parents always ask me: what's the inside scoop on this school?" Principal/teacher interviews, tours, parent perspectives.
- **College readiness** — "Want your teen to get ahead before college apps open?" Counselor/tutor interviews, student prep clips.
- **Neighborhood spotlights** — "Thinking of moving here? Here's what it's really like." Walking/drone tours: commute, safety, lifestyle.
- **Walkability & bike-friendliness** — "Could you do your whole day here without a car?" POV day-in-the-life routes.

## "Maybe" content (raw lines worth keeping)

- "You'll stop chasing clients and start attracting them."
- Ideal client profile dimensions: Demographics (age, gender, income, occupation, location, family status); Psychographics (motivations, fears, desires, values, interests, lifestyle, personality); Real estate profile (first-time buyers, downsizers, investors); Pain points (days on market, finding the next home, mortgage process, finances, past bad agent); Communication style (text/email/phone; visual vs step-by-step vs detail).
- "Nothing builds trust and awareness that compounds like a strong brand."
- Brand marketing as a ladder with five rungs: Identify, Attract, Engage, Convert, Retain.
- Coca-Cola doesn't run a "25% weekend flash sale." They tap emotion so you think of Coke at the supermarket.
- Geico: you won't switch right after an ad, but at renewal you remember "15 minutes could save you 15%."
- Measuring brand: Reach (how many in your market know you), Influence (how likely your presence changes behavior), Direction (do they move toward or away when ready to sell).
- "Your job: get more homeowners in your market to know you and move toward you when they begin to think about selling."
