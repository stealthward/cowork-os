## Draft (outline form)

### Become the trusted guide on the customer journey

- **Lead (Consideration):** someone with a problem you can solve who has shown interest in solving it.
- **Prospect (Decision):** expresses genuine interest in having you solve the problem, and meets your criteria (qualified).
- **Client (Retention).**
- Define the client journey and the audience segments in each stage.

**Convert:** What gets them to choose you? Demonstrate your ability to solve their problem and get better results than anyone else.

### Client journey stages

**Awareness.** A person becomes aware of a problem that's preventing them from getting what they want. Objective: make prospects aware they have a problem and show them how life improves when it's fixed. Strategy: make the pain feel urgent enough that they seek solutions; use peer success to spark curiosity.

**Consideration.** The prospect begins exploring ways to fix their problem. Objective: demonstrate you provide the most effective solution. Strategy: educate on what makes an effective approach, then prove you deliver it better, faster, and easier; remove uncertainty with process clarity, quality outcomes, and client satisfaction.

**Decision.** The prospect has decided to fix their problem. Objective: offer to fix it and encourage them to act now. Strategy: make the offer crystal clear, show proof of value, make it easy to book; use urgency, exclusivity, or added value.

**Retention.** The client keeps choosing you for every transaction. (Source text is written for RTM/Five Star: rewrite for the agent reader.)

**Advocacy.** The "referral flywheel." (Stub in source.)
