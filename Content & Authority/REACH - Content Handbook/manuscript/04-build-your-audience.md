## Draft

*"If you want to build a personal brand, make sure you spend time building an audience."* — Mario Peshev

### You Can't Create Demand for Real Estate

Your brand only works when people see it, and that means building an audience. But audiences in real estate behave differently than in most businesses, and it starts with one hard truth.

Real estate is all about positioning.

No ad campaign makes someone say, "You know what? I think I'll sell my house today." People move when life moves. Sometimes it's planned: their youngest graduates, they're ready to downsize, or a house finally hits the market in their dream neighborhood. Sometimes it isn't planned at all. A divorce, a death, or a job change forces the decision.

No amount of marketing makes those things happen. So you can't create demand in real estate. What you can do is position yourself to capture it, so when someone is finally ready to sell, you're the first agent they think of.

Why? According to the National Association of Realtors, 81% of sellers list with the first agent they speak to. If they don't think of you first, that listing is going to someone else.

If you want people to think of you first, you need content. If you want **a lot** of them, you need an audience.

The rest of this chapter will show you how to do that. But first, let's start with what *not* to do.

### Why Most Agents Struggle to Build an Audience

Most agents create content. Very few create *good* content.

I define good content as **"interesting and relevant to the people who see it."** That's it.

Scroll through most agents' feeds and you'll see the same thing: listings, market updates, sales posts, and open house announcements. All real estate, all the time.

The problem is that unless someone is buying, selling, or thinking about it, they don't care. Real estate content isn't interesting or relevant to most of the people who follow you, so it gets ignored.

Most people aren't thinking about real estate all the time, but they are always online. If your content only appeals to people active in the market, you lose everyone else.

That's why so many agents struggle to build an audience. Every time you post something people don't care about, a few more stop paying attention. Too much real estate content pushes away the very people who could one day become your clients. They unfollow, they mute, they scroll past, and by the time they need an agent, you're no longer on their radar.

### Build an Audience That Converts

Your audience is simply **the people who see your content.** If you post, email, advertise, or show up anywhere online or in person, and someone notices, you have an audience.

But having an audience doesn't guarantee anything. An audience only matters when it's filled with the right people, the ones who:

1. Buy and sell homes in your market, or know people who do
2. Think of you first when they, or someone they know, are ready to buy or sell

The more people in your audience who meet those two criteria, the more homes you'll sell.

Views are not the goal. You're building a brand the right people recognize: the ones most likely to refer others or hire you themselves.

That's why the first step in developing your brand is to define your ideal client. Once you know *who* you want to attract, you can build a large audience of these people by creating content that's relevant to them ("good content").

### Make Content Your Ideal Clients Care About

When your content reflects the lifestyle, goals, interests, and community of your ideal client, they start to associate those things with you. That's brand recognition. You become someone they recognize and trust because you deliver something they value, not because you sell real estate.

Most of the people who follow you have no plans to buy or sell right now. They're busy living their lives. Then something shifts and they begin thinking about a new home or a lifestyle change. Eventually that thought becomes a plan, and the plan becomes a transaction.

You can't control when those moments happen, but you can control whether you're top of mind when they do.

That's why most of your content should speak to people who **aren't** in the market yet. You want to be relevant long before they make a move, so you're already positioned when the moment arrives. You'll be the agent they already know and trust, not a stranger they scroll past.

This broader audience, *people who fit your ideal client profile but aren't yet in the market*, is the target for most of your content. It's the largest group you'll reach and the one that fuels every future opportunity.

And remember, a huge portion of your business won't actually come from the people in your audience. It will come in the form of referrals from the people **they know**. That's why your goal is simple: be the first agent they think of when they, *or someone they know*, is ready to buy or sell.

The larger your audience of people who fit your ideal client profile, the greater your chance of being top of mind when those life events happen, whether it's their move or someone else's.

### How Different Relationships Feed Your Pipeline

The ideal clients in your market share similar traits, but they're not all the same. They fall into groups based on the relationship they have with you. Some know you well. Some know of you. Most don't know you at all.

Each group needs a different approach. The post that lands with a close friend will not land with someone you barely know. Every message should match the relationship. That's why we segment the audience into smaller groups based on how well they know you.

I use a simple model called the **Three Spheres**. Picture your audience as three circles that form your opportunity roadmap. Every person you know, or could know, sits in one of these:

1. **Sphere of Influence (SOI):** People who think of you first when real estate comes up.
2. **Sphere of Recognition (SOR):** People who know you, but *don't* think of you first.
3. **Sphere of Proximity (SOP):** People in your market who don't know you at all.

The rest of this chapter will show you how to work each sphere. We will start with your Sphere of Influence, then move outward to Recognition and Proximity so you can see how every relationship fits into your pipeline.

Statistically, 66% of listings come from referrals and previous clients. That's 2 out of every 3. Your biggest source of business, *by a long shot*, comes from **people you already know.**

## Section: Sphere of Influence (SOI)

### Quick Reality Check

The term "SOI" gets thrown around a lot in real estate training circles. In my experience, most definitions are way too broad to be useful.

You'll frequently hear things like: "Your SOI is everyone you know who knows you by name," "all the people you would stop and speak to in public," or "every contact in your phone."

I disagree. Knowing someone's name is not influence. Saying hello in public or having their phone number is not influence either. Influence is the ability to shape decisions and change behavior in others.

Working definition for this handbook:

**Sphere of Influence = The people who think of you first for real estate, would hire you, and would refer others.**

Your Sphere of Influence is the group of people who already trust you enough to give you the first shot. That's the bar. If you're not the first agent they think of, they're not in your SOI yet.

### Your SOI is All That Matters

Your SOI is not just the foundation of your business. It *is* your business.

Every major study says the same thing. Most real estate deals come from the people who already know and trust you. The data from the National Association of Realtors makes it clear:

- **66%** of listings come from **referrals** or **previous clients**.
- **81%** of sellers hire the **first agent** they talk to.

*Caption: "Two out of three listings start with people who already know you."*

Almost all listings come from people you know. If you're not the first agent they think of, someone else is.

### You Only Have Two Objectives

The strategy is simple. **Protect** your SOI by staying visible, relevant, and easy to remember. **Expand** your SOI by building relationships that move people into this sphere.

In practical terms, everything you create serves one of these objectives. It either protects your Sphere of Influence by keeping you top of mind with the people who already trust you. Or it expands your Sphere of Influence by moving people from the outer spheres closer to you until they're ready to hire and refer.

When you think about content through that lens, it filters out all the other noise. Every post, video, email, or story is either protecting (and nurturing) the relationships you already have or helping new ones move toward you. That's how you turn attention into trust and trust into clients.

For this section, we are staying focused on protection. We'll talk about how to expand your SOI later when we get into the Sphere of Recognition and the Sphere of Proximity. Right now, we're talking about the people who *already* feel good associating their name with yours.

These are the ones who would confidently say, "You should use *my* agent!" without thinking twice. This group typically includes family and close friends, past clients you served well, and trusted community connections who advocate for you when real estate is mentioned.

**Try this simple SOI test:** If a friend asked them for an agent today, would you be the first name they say? If yes, they're in your SOI. If not, they belong in another sphere for now, and your goal is to move them inward.

Let's talk about protecting the people who are already in.

### What "Protecting Your SOI" Really Means

Protecting your SOI means making sure the relationship never feels cold or distant. I can start to feel that way if you are constantly blasting them with real estate posts or pandering for referrals. In practice, it looks like this:

They see you often enough that your name comes to mind without effort. They consume content that adds something to their life, not just to your pipeline. The relationship feels current, not like someone they knew in another chapter of life. Your results and client stories show that you're active, capable, and reliable. Your tone makes it obvious you value their support, their business, and their referrals.

You don't need to "sell" to your SOI. You need to remind them you exist, that you're good at what you do, and that you're always in their corner.

### How Agents Lose SOI Deals (without realizing it)

Most agents don't lose SOI deals because a competitor markets better. They lose them to silence and inconsistency that looks like this:

- Long stretches of total silence.
- A "just sold" blast every few months, and nothing in between.
- Billboard posts that never answer a comment or message.
- A contact list gone cold and disorganized.

When people don't hear from you, they don't sit around wondering what happened to your real estate business. They just fill the gap with the next agent who shows up.

When you focus on protecting your SOI, that doesn't happen.

### SOI Content Guidelines

Before you worry about what to post or how often, use these guidelines to shape your overall strategy. With your SOI, it should feel more like communication than "content."

**Organic is better than polished.** Real moments beat perfect ones. Simple videos, quick photos, and clear language usually land better than overproduced, stiff content that feels like an ad.

**Consistency beats intensity.** Showing up weekly or every other week is better than dropping a big burst of content and then disappearing. Your SOI needs to see you often enough that you stay familiar.

**Blend personal and professional.** Your SOI should see both your life and your work. If they only see real estate, you turn into background noise. If they only see your personal life, they forget you're a serious professional.

**Treat content as a conversation, not a broadcast.** Reply to comments. Respond to DMs. Ask simple questions and acknowledge their answers. Content that feels like a two-way conversation builds stronger relationships.

**Show proof more than promises.** Short client stories, clear outcomes, and real wins protect your SOI better than generic lines about "great service" or "working hard for you." Let the proof do the talking.

**Takeaway.** The good news: content for your SOI is usually easier and more fun to create, because you're talking to people you already have a relationship with. That same group is the one that will send you most of your listings and referrals.

### Content That Actually Protects Your SOI

Protecting your SOI is not complicated. It's about showing up often enough, in a real way, that you stay top of mind as their real estate agent. Most of what your SOI sees from you should feel like it's coming from a trusted friend and reliable real estate pro. To make this easier, think of your content in a few simple buckets.

**Personal life snapshots.** Share real life: moments with family and friends, the local events you go to, the hobbies, sports, and causes you care about. This keeps you familiar and human, not just "the real estate person."

**Soft professional proof.** Show you're active without yelling "Look at me." Share a short client story with a clear before and after. Drop a quick lesson from a recent transaction. Mix clean "new listing," "in escrow," and "just sold" posts in with everything else. These are subtle reminders that you help people like them.

**Helpful homeowner tips.** Give simple advice that makes their life easier, even when they're not moving. Send a seasonal maintenance reminder, or a small upgrade that adds comfort or long-term value. You want them thinking, "They're always looking out for us."

**Gratitude and recognition.** Say thanks in a way that still feels like content. Appreciate repeat clients, celebrate long-time relationships, and acknowledge the reviews, referrals, and introductions people send your way. You're showing your audience how you treat people, even when you're just saying thank you.

When your SOI sees this kind of content, they see you as present, valuable, and trustworthy without feeling like you're trying to sell them something.

### Turn a Listing Into a Content Run

When you have an active listing or a big client win, that's the perfect time to dial up the real estate content. The key is structure.

Instead of posting the same flyer three times, turn a transaction into a short, simple series.

**Start with the story.** Share who you helped, what they were trying to achieve, and why it mattered to them. Keep it human and specific.

**Show a piece of the process.** Give a quick behind-the-scenes moment, a challenge you had to solve, or a "here's how we handled X" lesson. This shows how you think, not just what you sold.

**Share the result.** Post the under-contract or just-sold moment and connect it back to the outcome the client cared about. Less "$100K over asking," more "They got to move closer to family."

**Pull out the education.** Record a fast tip or Q&A video with one thing you learned from that deal that would help a future seller or buyer. Teach them something useful.

Now one transaction becomes a focused content run instead of a single flyer. Your SOI sees your skill, your process, and how you take care of people, not just another templated graphic. In that window, you can lean a little heavier into real estate content because it's anchored in a real story.

### The Compounding Effect

Every meaningful touchpoint adds equity to your brand. One good video, post, or conversation can turn into multiple referrals because people in your SOI don't just think of you for their own needs. They think of you when someone else mentions buying or selling.

That's how referrals multiply.

Your SOI is not a static list. As you add new clients, neighbors, other parents, and community contacts, more people start thinking of you first. The bigger that group gets, and the more often you show up for them, the more your results compound year after year.

Your SOI is not just your starting point. It is your compounding engine. Protect it, grow it, and it will feed your pipeline for your entire career.

**Broadening Your Reach.** Once your Sphere of Influence is secure, the next opportunity is the group that already knows you but doesn't naturally turn to you first for real estate. That's your Sphere of Recognition. It's where tomorrow's SOI comes from.

## Section: Sphere of Recognition (SOR)

The people who recognize you by face or name but don't associate you with real estate. These are the familiar faces: old classmates, local business owners, parents at your child's school, or anyone who might say, "Oh yeah, I've seen you around."

**Goal:** Create content that builds association. You want them to connect with you on a deeper level and begin to see you as a trusted real estate resource.

Your Sphere of Recognition is your biggest growth opportunity.

These are the people who already know who you are. They recognize your face, your name, or your voice. They might see your posts, wave when you pass by, or know you through your kids' school, the gym or a local business.

They may even know you're in real estate, but they don't think of you first. At best, you're just another agent they know of.

That's the gap this sphere represents.

There's already familiarity, but no priority. You're known, but not yet remembered when it matters most. The goal here is to change that, to move people from recognition to influence.

You do that by building association and relevance through content.

### Why This Group Matters

This is where most of your growth will come from. The people in your Sphere of Recognition already have awareness, which means the hardest part of marketing, *getting noticed*, is already done.

You don't need to introduce yourself. You just need to connect your name to what you do and make it stick. When people in this group think about real estate, you want to be the first person who comes to mind.

That's what consistent, meaningful content does. It turns general awareness into professional association.

### How to Move People from Recognition to Influence

The best approach here is to **pull them in with general interest content and sprinkle in real estate.** Your goal is to create relevance through shared interests, not just property talk.

> **Pull Them In**
> - **Show lifestyle overlap.** Post about local schools, community events, restaurants, and hobbies you genuinely enjoy. These create natural bridges to people who live similar lives.
> - **Be part of the community.** Volunteer, attend local fundraisers, support neighborhood initiatives, and share those moments. It connects you to causes people care about.
> - **Blend personal and professional naturally.** Mix community and lifestyle topics with subtle real estate touchpoints like quick market insights, behind-the-scenes clips, or client success stories.
> - **Stay consistent and visible.** People notice repetition. Every post, story, and video reinforces familiarity and builds trust.
> - **Engage with their content.** Recognition works both ways. Comment, share, and interact with people in your area so they see you as part of their circle, not an ad in their feed.

### The Shift You're Creating

Your job isn't to convince this group to buy or sell right now. It's to earn the position of being their agent before they ever need one.

When you show up consistently in content that feels familiar and relevant, people start to associate you with what's meaningful to them, their family, their community, their lifestyle. And once that connection forms, you're no longer "just another agent." You're *their* agent.

That's when someone moves from the Sphere of Recognition into your Sphere of Influence.

## Section: Sphere of Proximity (SOP)

The Sphere of Proximity is where you build relationships with people who don't know you at all.

It's your larger community, the residents, business owners, and local leaders who live and work in your market but have no personal connection to you yet.

This is an advanced phase of audience building. Once you've protected your Sphere of Influence and created meaningful content for your Sphere of Recognition, you'll have the skills and confidence to expand outward. The goal here is to build relationships that bring new people into your world, not to sell to strangers.

### Relationship First, Audience Second

Your Sphere of Proximity is filled with potential allies. They already have trust and attention within your community. Business owners, nonprofit directors, school administrators, team coaches, event organizers, and other local voices are the people who shape your market's conversation.

The best way to connect with them is to create value for them first, before you ask for anything.

When you highlight their work, promote their cause, or help tell their story, you build genuine relationships that matter. In return, you earn credibility and exposure to their audience. That's how strangers start to recognize you, not as a salesperson, but as a connector.

### What This Looks Like

Here are a few ways agents can reach their Sphere of Proximity through collaborative, story-driven content:

> - **On-location spotlights:** Feature local businesses, organizations, or community projects that align with your brand values.
> - **Podcasts and interviews:** Sit down with community figures to share ideas, experiences, or local insight.
> - **Event coverage:** Attend local fundraisers, school events, or city gatherings and use short videos or reels to highlight the people involved.
> - **Partnership features:** Co-create content that brings value to both audiences, such as a small business owner offering home improvement tips or a charity sharing local impact stories.

Each of these builds connection, expands recognition, and helps move new people into your Sphere of Recognition.

### The Long Game

The Sphere of Proximity is where leadership begins. When you consistently create content that supports your community, you start to become part of its story. Over time, those collaborations and shared moments turn strangers into allies, and allies into clients and referral partners.

It takes time, intention, and consistency. But once you reach this level, your content stops working in isolation. It starts working as a network.

That's when your brand begins to compound beyond your personal reach.

## Section: The 3 Spheres Summary

Everything you post, record, or share fits into one of two purposes: **Protect your relationships** or **pull new people into them.** (Protect your SOI or Expand your SOI.)

That's the binary strategy behind audience building.

**Sphere of Influence (SOI).** Your inner circle. People who already know you and think of you first when real estate comes up. **Goal:** Protect this group with consistent, authentic content that reminds them who you are and what you do.

**Sphere of Recognition (SOR).** Your growth zone. People who know you but don't associate you with real estate. **Goal:** Pull them in with community, lifestyle, and interest-based content that builds connection and relevance.

**Sphere of Proximity (SOP).** Your expansion zone. People in your market who don't know you yet. **Goal:** Build relationships with community influencers and organizations through collaborative content that earns trust and broadens your reach.

### The Big Picture

When you master the three spheres, your content stops being random posts and starts becoming a system. Each piece serves a clear purpose: to stay visible, build trust, and move people closer to working with you.

That's how you create a brand that compounds, one relationship, one post, and one connection at a time.
