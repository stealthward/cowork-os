## Book promise

Become the first agent people think of when they, or someone they know, is ready to buy or sell. Do it by building a brand, an audience, and a content habit that compound.

## Sequence logic

Each part assumes the reader has what the last part gave them. Each chapter ends by naming the limit the reader just hit, and the next chapter is the fix for it. This is the "Next Up" pattern.

## Chapter template (every chapter)

1. Title, plus a "How to..." subtitle. The Doc tabs already have Subtitle placeholders.
2. Epigraph. Brett's own line is preferred over a borrowed quote.
3. Cold open: a client story at a moment of struggle. The chapter's framework resolves it later.
4. Scope line: "This chapter covers..."
5. One framework. Show it as a numbered list first, then expand each piece under its own heading.
6. Signposts:
   - Bottom line, after each major piece.
   - Action step, after each step.
   - Pro Tip ("how we do it at Real Talk"), from Brett's idea dump. It teaches; it does not pitch.
7. Objections.
8. "How you know it's working": benchmarks, only where Brett can source them.
9. Action items checklist.
10. Next Up: name the limit, then name the next chapter.

## Book-level devices

- **Running cast:** three or four agent clients at different stages, who come back across chapters. Readers watch peers win. Brett's authority comes from guiding them.
- **Spine visual:** the flywheel diagram, reprinted at each part opener with a "you are here" marker.
- **Orientation aids:** each part opens with a preview and closes with a recap, and Chapter 11 ends with a one-page recap of the whole book.
- **Visual-heavy pages:** short attention spans, so models, checklists, and before-and-after examples.
- **Back matter:** a light resource list only. The book teaches; it does not pitch.
