Nobody wakes up wanting an agent. They wake up with a change in their life, and the house no longer fits it.

The agent they call is the one who was already in the picture when that change happened.

This chapter covers three things. First, the path a person walks from a life change to hiring an agent. Second, the content each of your three spheres needs at each step on that path. Third, how to tell where a person is, so you say the right thing at the right time.

I call that path the **Realtor Client Journey**. It's one journey, and it works the same for buyers and sellers. You'll see an example of each inside every stage.

## Why people choose you

In Chapters 4 and 5, you built an audience and sorted it into three spheres. Now we turn that audience into clients.

So what gets someone to choose you? You show that you can solve their problem and that you get a better result than anyone else. The key word is *show*. "I get great results" is a claim, and every agent makes it.

Timing matters as much as proof. According to the National Association of Realtors, about 81% of sellers list with the first agent they speak to (8 of 10). [NAR source and edition to confirm]

A seller speaks first to the agent who comes to mind. That means the listing goes to the agent they think to call.

The work that makes you that agent happens long before the call. That work is content, matched to the stage the person is in.

## The Realtor Client Journey

Every client walks the same path. What changes is the content they need. That depends on two things: where they are on the path, and how well they already know you.

Here's the path.

| Stage | Where they are | Who they are to you |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Something in their life changes, by choice or by circumstance, and their home no longer fits. | Not yet a lead |
| 2. Consideration | They start exploring their options: stay or move, buy or sell, when, and who can help. | Lead |
| 3. Decision | They're ready to act and choose who will guide them. | Prospect |
| 4. Retention | They close, and you stay their agent for every move after. | Client |
| 5. Referrals | They send you the next person. | Client who refers |

This chapter covers the first three stages in full. Stages 4 and 5 get a short bridge at the end, and Chapter 7 takes them from there.

Your sphere sets the job of the content, using the binary strategy from Chapter 5.

- **Sphere of Influence (SOI):** these people already think of you first. Your content **protects** that, so you stay the call when life changes.
- **Sphere of Recognition (SOR):** these people know your face or your name, but they don't think of you first. Your content **expands** your SOI by tying your face to real estate.
- **Sphere of Proximity (SOP):** these people are in your market and don't know you yet. Your content **expands** your SOI by earning a stranger's attention and trust.

Three stages and three spheres make nine kinds of content. Here's the whole grid in one place.

| | SOI (protect) | SOR (expand) | SOP (expand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Stay present in their life so you hear about the change early. | Show up where their life is changing. | Name the circumstance so strangers recognize themselves. |
| **Consideration** | Answer their questions one to one before they search. | Prove you do this well. | Educate and earn trust. |
| **Decision** | Make the ask personal and easy. | Give a clear offer, proof, and one easy next step. | Give your strongest proof and the lowest-friction first step. |

## Stage 1: Awareness

**At the Awareness stage, something in a person's life changes, by choice or by circumstance, and their home no longer fits.**

They don't call it a real estate problem yet. They call it a new baby, a hard year, or a new job. The person isn't yet a lead, because they haven't shown interest in solving anything.

### What triggers Awareness

Awareness comes from life, not from wanting a new house. Some triggers are chosen. Others show up uninvited.

| Chosen | Unchosen |
|---|---|
| Marriage | Divorce |
| A new baby | A death in the family |
| A new job | Job loss or relocation |
| Retirement | Rising costs |
| An empty nest | Health changes |

Both columns matter, and the second one needs more care. A person facing divorce or a loss doesn't want a sales pitch. They want to know someone is steady and kind, and that you'll still be there when they're ready.

### Content for your SOI (protect)

Your goal is to stay present in their life so you hear about the change early. This content is personal and has no ask.

Send check-ins, congratulations, and life-event touches. Share stories about people they know who made a move and how it went.

*Buyer example:* A friend in your SOI gets engaged. You send a note, and that note keeps you close enough to be one of the first people she talks to when she and her fiancé start thinking about a place of their own.

*Seller example:* A longtime friend's last child heads to college. A short, warm message from you opens the door to a talk about what he wants his next chapter to look like.

### Content for your SOR (expand)

Your goal is to show up where their life is changing, and to connect your face with the moment. These people know you, but not for real estate.

Make content for the life stages you can speak to with confidence: a growing family, an empty nest, or a relocation. Loss is a moment to be present for, not a topic for a post. For loss, divorce, and health, stay steady and kind, use no sales language, and never aim content at someone's grief.

*Buyer example:* A short video on what renters in your market should know before their lease ends and they begin thinking about owning.

*Seller example:* A short post for homeowners whose kids have moved out, on what to think about before deciding whether to stay in a large house.

### Content for your SOP (expand)

Your goal is to name the circumstance so strangers recognize themselves. They don't know you, so the content has to be local and it has to describe their situation.

Problem-naming content works best. "Signs your home no longer fits your life" is a good example. A stranger sees their own kitchen in it and keeps watching.

The same care rule applies to strangers. Name life stages and everyday frustrations, never a stranger's loss, divorce, or health.

*Buyer example:* "What a growing family should ask before shopping for a bigger home in [your area]."

*Seller example:* "Four signs your house takes more upkeep than your life has room for."

### How to tell

They talk about the life change, not the house. You hear about the new job, the baby, a move for work, or a hard year, and nobody mentions price or listings. Listen for life changes, and let people share only what they choose.

**Bottom line:** People who don't know they have a real estate problem can't hire you to solve it. Your job at this stage is to be present and to name what's happening, with no ask.

**Action step:** Write down 10 people in your SOI. Next to each name, write the last life change you know about and how you'll reach out this week.

## Stage 2: Consideration

**At the Consideration stage, a person starts exploring their options: stay or move, buy or sell, when, and who can help.**

This is where a person becomes a **lead**. A lead is someone with a problem you can solve who has shown interest in solving it.

Consideration is the stage of questions. How much is my home worth? Is now a good time? What would it take to get ready?

The agent who answers well earns a place on the short list.

### Content for your SOI (protect)

Your goal is to answer their questions one to one, before they search for the answers on their own. At this stage, a personal reply is content.

Send home value updates, timing advice, and market updates by email or direct message. Keep each one short and aimed at one person.

*Seller example:* A past neighbor asks if now is a good time to sell. You send back a plain explanation of what you're seeing in her area and offer to go deeper if she wants.

*Buyer example:* A friend's son is a year from buying. You send him a short note on what lenders look at, and how to start early.

### Content for your SOR (expand)

Your goal is to prove you do this well. These people have seen you, so now show your work.

Share wins, your process, behind-the-scenes looks, and client stories. Let the work speak. When you post "Here's how I prepare a home for market," you show more than you do with "I'm great at selling homes."

*Buyer example:* A walk-through of how you help a buyer write an offer that stands out in your market.

*Seller example:* A before-and-after of the steps you recommend to a seller before the house goes live.

### Content for your SOP (expand)

Your goal is to educate and earn the trust of people who have never met you.

Publish buyer and seller guides, FAQs, market explainers, and reviews. Teach the way you'd teach a friend.

A person who understands the process chooses on facts. A person who doesn't understand it chooses on a familiar name or the lowest price.

*Buyer example:* A guide to the buying process in your area, from pre-approval to keys.

*Seller example:* A plain-English explainer on how pricing works and what to expect in the first 30 days on the market.

### How to tell

They ask questions. "What's my home worth?" and "Is now a good time?" are Consideration questions.

**Bottom line:** At this stage, people are looking for the answer first and the agent second. If you teach well and show proof, you become both.

**Action step:** Start a proof folder. Save every kind word, review, and finished outcome in it the day it arrives, with permission.

## Stage 3: Decision

**At the Decision stage, a person is ready to act and chooses who will guide them.**

This is the stage most agents talk to all day. It's also where a clear ask belongs.

### Lead, prospect, client

Three words track a person along the journey, and each one belongs to a stage.

- **Lead (Consideration):** someone with a problem you can solve who has shown interest in solving it.
- **Prospect (Decision):** a lead who shows genuine interest in having *you* solve it and who meets your criteria.
- **Client (Retention):** a person who hired you.

Your content moves people from the first to the third.

### The qualified prospect

It's fine to have criteria. A **qualified prospect** is ready, willing, able, and a fit.

- **Ready:** they have a real timeline.
- **Willing:** they're motivated to act.
- **Able:** a buyer is pre-approved. A seller has equity.
- **A fit:** they're your ideal client, in your market.

This list protects them as much as it protects you. A person who isn't ready, willing, able, or a fit is better served by a different plan, or a different agent.

### Content for your SOI (protect)

Your goal is to make the ask personal and easy. These people already trust you, so the content is a direct invitation to talk.

A personal message works better than a broadcast. Try something like, "If you're thinking about a move this year, I'd love to talk it through with you."

*Seller example:* A past client mentions a transfer. You reply with an offer to sit down and walk through timing and next steps.

*Buyer example:* A friend asks about buying. You offer a conversation about her budget, her timeline, and what to do first.

### Content for your SOR (expand)

Your goal is to give a clear offer, proof, and one easy next step. They know of you. Now they need to see exactly what working with you looks like.

State your offer in plain words: what you do, for whom, and what happens next. "Let's chat" leaves people guessing. "A 30-minute conversation about your plan and what to expect" is an offer.

*Buyer example:* A post that describes your buyer consultation, what you cover, and how to book it.

*Seller example:* A short video showing what a seller receives from you in the first meeting, with a link to book.

### Content for your SOP (expand)

Your goal is to show your strongest proof and the lowest-friction first step. A stranger who is ready still needs a reason to trust you and a small, safe way to start.

Lead with your best reviews and outcomes. Then offer a consult or a home valuation, with one link and one clear action.

*Seller example:* A home valuation offer, paired with a client review and a simple booking link.

*Buyer example:* A free buyer consult, paired with a review from a buyer who started in the same place.

### How to tell

They ask about you. They want to know your process, your availability, and your fee.

**Bottom line:** A person who has decided needs a clear offer and an easy next step. When both are easy to find, they never have to wonder what to do.

**Action step:** Write your offer in one sentence: what you do, for whom, and the next step. Then write your qualified-prospect criteria: what ready, willing, able, and a fit look like for you.

## Retention and Referrals: the bridge to Chapter 7

The journey doesn't end at the closing. Two more stages come after Decision.

**Retention** is the client who keeps choosing you for every move after the first, theirs and their family's.

**Referrals** is the client who sends you the next person.

A referral works because it does Stages 1 through 3 for you. A friend names the problem, vouches for you, and sends the person to your door. About 66% of listings come from referrals and previous clients (2 of 3), according to the NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers. [NAR edition to confirm]

There's a gap to close. About 88 to 89% of clients say they'd use or refer their agent again, and fewer than 20% actually do. [source TBD, verify before use]

Each referral story becomes Awareness content for the next person whose life is changing. That loop is the **referral flywheel**, and Chapter 7 shows how to build it.

**Bottom line:** The first closing is the start of the next one.

**Action step:** List your last five clients. Next to each name, write one way you'll reach out in the next 30 days.

## Action items

One for each stage, plus the bridge. Each one fits in a single sitting.

1. **Awareness: list 10 people in your SOI and their last life change.**
   - How to do it: Write the name, the change you know about, and how you'll reach out this week.
   - Why it's important: You can't protect a relationship you aren't paying attention to.
   - Example: A congratulations note to a friend who just changed jobs.

2. **Consideration: start a proof folder.**
   - How to do it: Save every kind word, review, and finished outcome in one place the day it arrives, with permission.
   - Why it's important: Proof is what makes Consideration content work.
   - Example: A screenshot of a client message, saved the day it arrives.

3. **Decision: write your offer in one sentence and your qualified-prospect criteria.**
   - How to do it: Say what you do, for whom, and the next step. Then define ready, willing, able, and a fit for your business.
   - Why it's important: A clear offer is the heart of Decision content, and criteria tell you who it's for.
   - Example: "A 30-minute conversation about your plan and what to expect."

4. **Bridge: list your last five clients and plan one touch for each in the next 30 days.**
   - How to do it: Write each name and one way you'll reach out.
   - Why it's important: The first closing is the start of the next one.
   - Example: A short note on the anniversary of their closing.
