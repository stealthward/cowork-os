## The framework: the Realtor Client Journey, by sphere

One journey. Every client walks the same path. What changes is the content they need, and that depends on two things: where they are on the path, and how well they already know you.

| Stage | Where they are | Who they are to you |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Something in their life changes, by choice or by circumstance, and their home no longer fits. | Not yet a lead |
| 2. Consideration | They start exploring their options: stay or move, buy or sell, when, and who can help. | Lead: someone with a problem you can solve who has shown interest in solving it |
| 3. Decision | They are ready to act and choose who will guide them. | Prospect: shows genuine interest in having you solve it and meets your criteria |
| 4. Retention (Ch 7) | They close, and you stay their agent for every move after. | Client |
| 5. Referrals (Ch 7) | They send you the next person. | Client who refers |

The sphere tells you the content's job (binary strategy):
- **SOI** already thinks of you first. Content **protects** that, so you are the call when life changes.
- **SOR** knows your face or name but does not think of you first. Content **expands** your SOI by tying your face to real estate.
- **SOP** does not know you yet. Content **expands** your SOI by earning a stranger's attention and trust.

### The grid (one line per cell; examples are placeholders for Brett)

| | SOI (protect) | SOR (expand) | SOP (expand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Stay present in their life so you hear about the change early: personal check-ins, life-event touches, stories of people they know who moved. | Show up where their life is changing: life-stage content (growing family, empty nest, relocation, loss) that links you to the moment. | Name the circumstance so strangers recognize themselves: local, problem-naming content ("signs your home no longer fits"). |
| **Consideration** | Answer their questions one to one before they search: home value, timing, market updates by email or DM. | Prove you do this well: wins, process, behind the scenes, client stories. | Educate and earn trust: buyer and seller guides, FAQs, market explainers, reviews. |
| **Decision** | Make the ask personal and easy: a direct invitation to talk. | A clear offer, proof, and one easy next step. | Your strongest proof and the lowest-friction first step (a consult, a home valuation). |

## Answers locked (Brett, 2026-11-04; grid approved the same day)

- **Awareness triggers.** Chosen: marriage, new baby, new job, retirement, empty nest. Unchosen: divorce, death in the family, job loss or relocation, rising costs, health changes.
- **How to tell.** Awareness: they talk about the life change, not the house. Consideration: they ask questions (what's my home worth, is now a good time). Decision: they ask about you: your process, your availability, your fee.
- **Qualified prospect: ready, willing, able, and a fit.** Ready: a real timeline. Willing: motivated to act. Able: a pre-approved buyer or a seller with equity. A fit: your ideal client and your market.
- **Retention:** the client who keeps choosing you for every move after the first, theirs and their family's.
- **Referrals:** the client who sends you the next person.
- **Loss (amends the Awareness x SOR cell):** loss stays an Awareness trigger but is not a content topic; for loss, divorce, and health, stay steady and kind with no sales language (Brett, 2026-11-04).
- **Teaching statements:** Brett's views on how buyers, sellers, and agents behave stay as plain statements with no opinion labels; only figures need a source (Brett, 2026-11-04).
