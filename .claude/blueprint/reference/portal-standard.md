## Principles

- Market plus service pairing explicit.
- The opening paragraph determines authority perception.
- Proof concrete and verifiable.
- Risk reduction increases trust and conversion.
- The bio reflects the actual business model.
- Consistent across platforms but not identical word for word.
- First person unless asked otherwise.
- Supports entity clarity and recommendation-readiness.

**Priorities (Playbook section 19):** specificity over polish, proof over claims, clarity over personality, consistency over novelty. A strong bio makes it immediately clear who the agent serves, where they work, why they are credible, and why the client can trust the process.

## Required inputs

**Audit:** portal URL or bio text, market, transaction focus, proof points, business model (evaluate what is visible and note gaps).
**Creation:** markets; transaction focus (seller-first, buyer-first, balanced, luxury, relocation, investor, niche); business model (solo, team lead, team-based, brokerage owner); differentiators; proof points; risk reducer (direct access, structured team support, defined process, specialized role clarity); audience, niche, geography, or property type to emphasize; voice reference. Materially incomplete: stop and ask (engagement). A test run uses labeled best guesses instead (blueprint-portal-bio SKILL.md).

## Audit framework

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| A. Positioning clarity | Market explicit? Transaction focus clear? Agent's role understandable? Generic or specific? |
| B. Authority signaling | Proof visible or buried? Reviews, experience, production, specialization used well? |
| C. Differentiation | A real, concrete edge, or generic realtor copy? |
| D. Risk reduction | Is uncertainty reduced? Service model clear? Does the client know how the experience is handled? |
| E. Consistency | Do the portals tell the same story, with the same proof? Does one contradict another? |
| F. Search and AI readiness | Service plus geography paired? Easy to summarize? Specific enough to be recommended? |

Audit output: what is weak, inconsistent, generic, or underused; where proof is missing or buried; where market or service clarity is weak; where profiles contradict each other; what to fix first.

## Writing standard (blueprint-portal-bio)

**Length.** 175 to 200 words unless asked otherwise.

**Shape (Brett, 2026-10-30: Playbook opening, StoryBrand body).** Four short paragraphs, as in the example:

1. **Opening paragraph (Playbook section 7).** Sentence 1: market plus role plus transaction focus. Sentence 2: differentiator plus proof. Sentence 3: risk reducer or service model ("When you hire me, you work directly with me"). A fourth sentence may carry one human detail that explains how the agent works (the example's Navy pilot line). Never a checklist.
2. **The client's side (StoryBrand).** The client is the hero and the agent the guide: name what the client is up against in this market, then the agent's role in plain terms (clarity, preparation, steady guidance) and one line of empathy or care. Short, warm, no fear tactics.
3. **How it works.** The service model or process in one or two sentences. A three-step process only when it helps, derived from the agent's real workflow (Preparation, Positioning, Negotiation; never "clarity, strategy, execution"). Do not force it into every bio.
4. **Soft close.** One sentence, low pressure, no hype ("If you are buying or selling along North County Coastal, I would be glad to help.").

**Business model.** Never assume seller-first, hyperlocal, solo, team, backstory, or media presence. Adapt to the real model.

**Proof.** Two to four signals (years, volume, transactions, local transactions, reviews, certifications, recognized authority, media, specialization), strongest early. Never fabricate; prefer ranges; avoid exact numbers that date quickly unless provided and approved.

**Risk reducers**, only if true and specific: personally leads each transaction, structured team oversight, dedicated listing and buyer specialists, defined communication standards, systems-driven execution, high-touch advisory process, controlled exposure strategy, defined niche expertise. Never "client-focused", "dedicated to service", "committed to excellence".

**Differentiators**, concrete: marketing-based, negotiation-based, data-based, network-based, process-based.

**Variance across portals (Playbook section 13).** Identical core positioning, proof, and service model. Vary phrasing and flow by the allowed methods only: reorder proof elements, adjust sentence rhythm, modify process wording, slightly vary the closing line, adjust emphasis between differentiator and proof. The versions are recognizably the same bio, as in the example. Never change a fact between portals. Shared phrasing is fine; a no-repeat rule is not part of this standard.

**Tone.** Professional, clear, confident but controlled. Luxury understated; high-volume teams structured and decisive; community-driven warmer. No hype, clichés, exaggerated claims, or generic realtor language.

**Consistency.** Bios align with the website, Google Business Profile, social bios, brokerage profile, and the Blueprint's positioning.

## Checklist

Market plus service explicit in sentence 1; transaction focus clear; business model accurate; strongest proof early; differentiator concrete; risk reducer specific and true; service model clear; the client is the hero in the body; reads as flowing prose, not a checklist or a template; entity clarity and recommendation-readiness supported; consistent with other profiles; versions vary phrasing, not facts; nothing fabricated; no generic realtor language; no em dashes.
