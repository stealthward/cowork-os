## Core philosophy

Demand in real estate cannot be created, only positioned for. Content builds memory, memory drives first contact, first contact often wins the listing. Build the audience with content it already cares about; drip professional real estate content 20 to 25 percent of the time; keep social consistent with the rest of the public footprint.

## Audit framework (social and content, beyond the post-level pull)

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| A. Positioning clarity | Clear who the agent is for? Authority anchor visible? Market or community relevance clear? |
| B. Content balance | 75/25 roughly present? Real estate content overused? Authority under-reinforced? |
| C. Sphere coverage | Protecting the SOI, building association with the SOR, expanding into the SOP? |
| D. Authority reinforcement | Competence demonstrated? Proof visible? Authority or just activity? |
| E. Referral and retention | Visible to past clients and referral sources? Retention logic? Closing the willingness-to-refer gap? |
| F. Channel roles | Platforms used intentionally? Website, portals, Google, and social tell one story? |
| G. Memorability | Real association with a category, market, or audience? Distinct enough to matter? |
| H. Public brand consistency | Social bios repeat the website and portal service plus geography pairing? Easier to understand and recommend? |

Audit output (playbook 18A): concise findings; what is weak, missing, generic, or misaligned; how content supports or weakens authority, audience growth, and memorability; what to fix first.

## Authority anchors

Local authority, luxury strategist, negotiation specialist, data-driven advisor, community connector, investor-focused operator, relocation expert, move-up specialist, downsizing advisor. One primary anchor per agent.

## Professional content

Should demonstrate competence, reinforce activity, clarify process, show verifiable results, reduce perceived risk. Not flyer reposts, generic market stats, commission bragging, or production without insight. Listing run model: client story, strategic approach, behind-the-scenes insight, outcome, lesson for future sellers.

## Referral and retention engine

Retention: value-first emails, home anniversaries, client recognition, homeowner maintenance tips. Referral: subtle referral language, story-based referrals, social proof, client outcome lessons. Visibility: community features, shareable local insight, soft reminders of expertise, local collaborations.

## Platform roles

| Platform | Role |
|---|---|
| Instagram | Attention, local lifestyle relevance, light authority, familiarity |
| Facebook | Community dialogue, relationship maintenance, referral visibility |
| YouTube | Search-based authority, process education, deeper trust |
| Email and newsletter | Retention, repeat visibility, referral activation |
| LinkedIn | Professional credibility, referral network, relocation or executive reach |
| Podcast or long-form | Authority depth, relationship building, local trust |

## Content roadmap model (blueprint-strategy)

Ideal client profile; primary authority anchor; three-spheres strategy; three to five recurring pillars; channel roles; campaign direction; repurposing logic; conversion mechanism. High-level and outcome-focused; no post-by-post calendar unless asked.

## Checklist

Ideal client defined or limitation stated; anchor clear; 75/25 respected or the adjustment justified; spheres mapped; referral or retention logic present; matches the business model; channel roles intentional; website, portals, Google, and social not in conflict; nothing fabricated; no generic realtor language; no em dashes.
