## What the Blueprint must feel like (5.1)

A premium strategic report: more developed than a memo, more structured and scannable than a long-form analysis, substantial without bloat. Narrative carries the strategy; structured assets (tables, callouts, roadmap, numbered priority actions) let the client see the diagnosis, the opportunity, and the execution path at a glance. Never a prose-only memo. Never a bullet-only worksheet. The client should feel they are reading a polished strategic report, not planning notes.

## Required structure (5.1)

1. Report Objective
2. Executive Snapshot
3. Brand Diagnosis
4. Brand Direction
5. Channel Strategy
6. Visibility Strategy
7. Phased Action Plan
8. Closing Direction

All eight are required. Sub-headings inside each may be renamed to fit the client; the section's job may not change.

## Opening (5.2)

- **Title:** `[Client Name] Real Estate Branding Blueprint`. **Subtitle:** `Brand Positioning & Visibility Strategy for [Primary Market or Audience]`. Client-specific, never interchangeable.
- **Report Objective:** one or two sentences before the strategy that orient the client and set the frame. Model: "Clarify [Client]'s public brand position, align the digital footprint, and create a practical visibility system around [primary market or service focus]."

## Executive Snapshot (5.3)

Near the front. Makes the diagnosis and the core recommendation scannable and stops later sections from repeating them. Elements: Current Impression, Recommended Position, Primary Market, Primary Audience, Primary Brand, Core Opportunity, Highest-Leverage First Move. Add Desired Business Mix, Adjacent Markets, or Supporting Identity when discovery supplies them. Concise. In the delivered .docx it may also appear as stat tiles (Sean Zanganeh layout).

## Brand Diagnosis (5.4)

Must include: Executive Diagnosis; Business Model Summary; Ideal Client and Market Definition; Major Brand Gaps and Misalignments; Strategic Implications.

Answers: What business is this client actually building? What does the footprint communicate now? Where is the gap between perception and business reality? Why does that gap matter commercially?

Structured assets, one or more: Current State Scorecard; Core Problem callout; Primary Opportunity callout; Current vs Recommended Positioning table (rows such as primary identity, market focus, service focus, ideal client, differentiation, website role, portal role, social role, search impression). Enough narrative to explain the diagnosis; do not bury it in long paragraphs.

The diagnosis draws on the Internal Brand Audit and RTM Digital Brand Analysis. It does not re-run the audit; it states the finding and cites it.

## Brand Direction (5.5)

Must include: Positioning Direction; What the Client Should Become Known For; Core Differentiators; Brand Architecture Direction when relevant (agent vs team vs brokerage name, supporting identities); Messaging Direction; Example Messaging Territory.

Answers: What should the market consistently understand? What makes it believable? What to emphasize and de-emphasize? How to frame the brand going forward?

**Brand Platform table** (Element, Recommendation): Primary Brand, Primary Audience, Primary Market, Adjacent Markets, Core Promise, Proof Points, Tone; add Supporting Identity, Core Brand Idea, De-emphasize when relevant. It is the reference the client approves and judges future execution against. Narrative explains why the direction works; the Platform holds the repeated facts, so they are not re-explained.

Messaging territory lines are directional, labeled as not final, and restrained. Differentiators must come from discovery or Confirmed facts, never inferred.

## Channel Strategy (5.6)

Must include: Website Role; Portal and Profile Role; Google Business Profile Role; Social Media Role; Email, Newsletter, Podcast, Video, or Other Media Role when relevant; Cross-Channel Consistency Direction.

Answers: What role does each channel play? Which are underperforming or unclear? Which need alignment first? How does the story become consistent? Which supporting standard guides execution (portal-standard.md, website-standard.md, content-standard.md)?

**Channel Role Matrix** (Channel, Role, Priority, Content or Messaging Focus), one row per channel the agent has or should have. Inactive channels get a cleanup row (remove, unpublish, or unlink). The matrix carries channel-by-channel clarity; the narrative explains why the system works and does not restate rows.

Social roles come from content-standard.md (platform roles, 75/25, Three Spheres, referral and retention engine) and the post-level social audit. Not a content calendar, not a technical website plan.

## Visibility Strategy (5.7)

Must include: Public Search Impression Direction; Google Business Profile Direction; AI Recommendation-Readiness Direction; Service Plus Geography Standardization; Authority and Proof Visibility Direction; Relationship, Referral, and Recognition Strategy when relevant.

Answers: How does the agent show up more clearly in discovery? What service plus geography language repeats? Which profiles need consistency? Which proof points lead? What makes the agent more recommendation-ready in Google, portals, and AI summaries?

Structured assets: **Visibility Flywheel** (recognition, search, validation, engagement, reinforcement; or the Playbook's six-step flow from referral or local recognition to opportunity); a **Service Plus Geography** phrase table (phrase territory, where it is used). Uses search-visibility.md findings. Never technical SEO: no backlinks, crawl, indexing, page speed, or algorithm speculation. No ranking claims unless observed, with where and when.

## Phased Action Plan (5.8)

Three phases by default (Clarify the Foundation; Align and Strengthen the Public Footprint; Build Authority and Visibility Over Time); more or renamed phases when it improves pacing.

**Roadmap Overview table** first (Phase, Focus, Highest-Leverage Move, Intended Outcome).

Each phase has three parts:
1. **Strategic Narrative:** why the phase matters and how it moves the strategy forward.
2. **Priority Actions:** numbered, in order of execution, specific enough to make the work tangible, not so granular it becomes a project plan. Weighted: primary moves first and marked as such, supporting moves after.
3. **Intended Outcome:** a short paragraph on what is true at the end of the phase.

**Phase 1 rule:** the most visible, highest-leverage, easiest moves first. Portal bio rewrites and profile cleanup go in Phase 1 when portals are weak, incomplete, inconsistent, or outdated and enough positioning exists. Also typical: Google Business Profile alignment, service plus geography standardization, proof clarification, profile consistency fixes, homepage headline cleanup. When `bios.md` or `site-copy.md` already exist and match the Blueprint, Phase 1 names them as ready to publish.

The plan sequences the work; it does not re-argue the strategy. Real Talk Media provides the Blueprint, so it is named as the agent's strategic content partner, giving the platforms continuity, frequency, and consistency (Brett, 2026-11-03); no pricing, packages, or sales language.

## Closing Direction (5.9)

Required, and a developed closing narrative, not a bullet wrap-up. Reinforces the direction, summarizes the path without repeating tactics, introduces no new tactics, and ends on a credible, positive end state: easier to understand, trust, recommend, and choose, aligned with the business the client is building. The client finishes clearer, more confident, and motivated to act.

**Recommended Next Step** callout near the close, one or two sentences (for example, "Approve the positioning direction and begin Phase 1 with portal rewrites and Google Business Profile alignment").

## Cover Email (Blueprint)

Short, client-facing, sent by Brett. Frames the Blueprint as the strategy that follows discovery, names the one decision the client makes next (the Recommended Next Step), and offers async review (comments on the document) before any call. No restated strategy. Brett's voice (voice-principles.md), since Brett sends it.

## Narrative progression (6)

Where you are now; the key problems and misalignments; what needs to change; the stronger position and business outcome you can achieve. Visible across the Blueprint and again in the Closing Direction.

## Redundancy control (6.1, 6.2)

Each section has one job:

| Section | Job |
|---|---|
| Brand Diagnosis | The current problem and why it matters |
| Brand Direction | The future position and message |
| Channel Strategy | The position applied to each platform |
| Visibility Strategy | Search, Google, AI, and recommendation-readiness implications |
| Phased Action Plan | Sequence, without re-arguing |
| Closing Direction | The end state, without restating tactics |

Pattern: define once, reinforce through structured assets, apply by channel, sequence in phases, summarize in closing. After the Snapshot, positioning table, or Brand Platform establishes a core idea (primary market, service priority, primary brand, supporting brokerage, website role, portal alignment, AI visibility, social role), later sections reference it briefly and move to application. Before finalizing, cut circular language and repeated explanations.

## Tone (6.3, 10)

Confident, composed, decisive, commercially intelligent, easy to follow without feeling simplified. Strategic, conclusive, aspirational, manageable. Not repetitive, instructional, academic, procedural, or over-explained.

- **Direct assertion:** no "It is not X. It is Y.", "The issue is not X. The issue is Y.", or "This does not mean X. It means Y." State the position directly.
- **No over-explaining:** avoid "this is important because", "this matters because", "this helps", "this allows". Assume the reader values clarity and consistency.
- **Sentence variety:** vary rhythm and construction across paragraphs.
- **Forward momentum:** restate an idea only to add an implication or apply it somewhere new.
- **Controlled emphasis:** expand the core ideas, compress the supporting ones.
- **Advisory voice:** direct statements and clear conclusions; no "this suggests", "this indicates", hedging, or tentative language.
- Say "potential" or "maximize the potential", never "what a property can become", and never "what the rules allow". "Zoning and local ordinances" is the phrase only when the agent's edge depends on what can be built; otherwise zoning stays out (Brett, 2026-11-03).
- Third person about the agent, written by Real Talk Media to the agent. No hype, no generic realtor adjectives, no em dashes ever.

## Integrity (11, 8)

integrity.md binds. Never fabricate metrics, proof, rankings, testimonials, differentiators, transaction history, outcomes, or business model claims. A missing input that materially affects strategy is asked for, never assumed; placeholders only for minor proof gaps. A public source that conflicts with what the client or Brett said is flagged with a recommendation to verify. Compliance (team-name advertising, license display, brokerage identification, fair housing): name the governing body, recommend broker review, never interpret.

## QA checklist (13, Playbook prompt 5)

Run before anything reaches Brett:

- [ ] All eight sections present; Closing Direction fully developed; Recommended Next Step present
- [ ] Executive Snapshot near the front
- [ ] Current vs Recommended Positioning, Brand Platform, Channel Role Matrix, Visibility Flywheel, and Roadmap Overview used where they add clarity
- [ ] Clear positioning and concrete differentiators, each traceable to discovery or a Confirmed fact
- [ ] Channel and visibility strategy defined and reflects search and AI findings; no technical SEO
- [ ] Website, portal, social, and Google positioning aligned with no conflicts, and consistent with `bios.md` and `site-copy.md` when they exist
- [ ] Phase 1 clear and actionable, portal updates in Phase 1 when they are quick wins; every phase has narrative, ordered Priority Actions, Intended Outcome
- [ ] Core ideas defined once then applied; repeated references to market, service focus, brand architecture, website role, and portal alignment shortened after first mention
- [ ] No contrast constructions; advisory tone; no em dashes
- [ ] Copy examples restrained and purpose-driven
- [ ] No fabricated proof, rankings, or claims; fact-checker re-run clean or open items named
- [ ] Ends on a positive, credible end state with clear implementation motion

## Delivered layout (.docx, Brett 2026-11-03: Sean Zanganeh's layout)

Contents page listing the eight sections. Executive Snapshot as a stat-tile grid (short value, small label) followed by the Snapshot table. Numbered section openers ("01 SECTION ONE", title). Tables with a header row; callouts as single-cell shaded boxes (Report Objective, Core Problem, Primary Opportunity, Recommended Next Step). Closing footer in italics: "Prepared by Real Talk Media. This document is confidential and intended solely for the named recipient. The strategy outlined in this blueprint reflects the current understanding of the business and is intended as a directional framework, not a guaranteed outcome."
