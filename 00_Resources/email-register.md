# Email Register — Brett Ward

Email-specific overlay to `voice-principles.md`. The base doc governs Tone, Sentence Structure, Words/Phrases to Avoid, and Word Choice. This file captures how Brett's **actual sent email** diverges from a faithful reading of the base doc, so drafts match his inbox voice — not generic-professional, and not his marketing/content voice.

**How to use:** Apply base voice for sentence mechanics. Apply *this* file for register, greeting/sign-off, length, and the email-only word choices below. When the two conflict on a reply, this file wins.

Built from a calibration pass on 15 personally-written Sent replies (May–June 2026) spanning team, clients/agents, vendors/finance, and one contentious client dispute. Each was stripped to intent, re-drafted blind from base voice, then compared. Deltas only below.

## Refinements (email-only rules the base doc doesn't capture)

### Greetings
- **Internal team (Felicia, Heather, Elizabeth, Mark, Cassie):** no salutation. Open on the substance or the decision word — "Okay, let's…", "Yes, I know…", "Just a small handful of findings…", "Morning." Do not write "Hi [Name],".
- **Groups:** collective address — "Gang,", "Hi friends…", or initials for show partners ("D&V-" for Danny & Valli).
- **External individuals (vendors, clients, solicitors):** first name or nickname + comma — "Hi Moriah,", "Syd,", "Jayson,". Brett shortens names (Syd, not Sydney).
- **John Casarietti (CFO, RSM):** "Thanks, Cass" opener or cold open. Never "John."
- **Terse vendor/support threads:** greeting optional; he'll open cold ("Does this relate…", "Hi, photos attached").

### Sign-offs
- Brett rarely types a closing. His WiseStamp signature auto-appends "Best," + block. **Do not manufacture "Thanks, Brett" or "Best, Brett" in the body.** End on the last substantive sentence. A bare "Thx!" or "Thank you both!" is fine when warmth is wanted; a formal sign-off is not his default.

### Length
- Default **shorter than the base doc implies.** Most replies are 1–3 sentences. Confirmations are 1–4 words ("Yes please", "confirmed, thx", "Very."). Only show-leadership and dispute emails run to paragraphs. When in doubt, cut.
- **Trusted internal people (Elizabeth, Cass) get the shortest replies of all.** Elizabeth example: "Please create an Asana task so we can sit down and discuss after you hear back from Carlos." No greeting, no sign-off, names the dependency, stops. Don't pad with reasoning she already has. (Capitalize sentence starts even when terse.)

### Punctuation tics
- **Trailing ellipsis** is a signature move as a casual connective/opener: "Awesome Valli…", "Hi friends…", "Just a small handful of findings…". Use sparingly but it reads as him.
- Base-doc **no-em-dash rule still holds.**

### Emojis
- Used freely **internally and with close partners** to carry tone: 😁 (light), 👊 (agreement/thanks), 🤬 (real frustration). **Never** with vendors, solicitors, disputes, or any compliance/finance/legal thread. Default off for external; on only when the relationship is warm and established.

### Drafting scope — convey intent only
- **Do not manufacture action items, next steps, or instructions to the recipient unless Brett states them.** Draft only his stated intent. When he says "tell them X," write X and stop. Do not add directives like "send me a few windows," "let me know," or "and I'll lock it in" on his behalf. If a next step seems needed, leave it out and let Brett add it.

### Reply-all default
- **When a thread has other people copied (multiple To recipients or any Cc), draft as reply-all by default.** Keep every original recipient on the reply so the group stays in the loop, unless Brett says to reply to the sender only. This applies to normal business and team threads. Solicitations get no draft at all, and disputes or sensitive compliance threads use judgment, since venting and corrections stay off the group thread per the dispute rule.

### Solicitation signals
- **Brett's name in the subject line is a strong solicitation signal.** Most of the time an email puts "Brett" in the subject, it is cold outreach. Treat it as a likely solicitation: if it shows other cold-pitch traits, route to @Sane/Solicitations; if otherwise ambiguous, flag it rather than auto-routing. Known contacts and active threads are exempt.

### Word choice — email exception
- In casual internal and peer email, Brett naturally uses **"leverage"** and **"circle back"** — both on the base-doc avoid-list. The avoid-list is calibrated for *marketing and content*, not quick email. **Don't police these out of low-stakes internal/peer replies if they fit naturally;** still avoid them in client-facing polished copy, brand descriptions, and anything a prospect or external party reads as representative of the company.
- Casual intensifiers are on-voice in email: "crazy good", "already amazing", "Super informative", "Awesome".
- "deeper dive", "took a peek", "dig in" are on-voice in email, even though "dive into" is banned in marketing/content copy.

### Register by recipient (tone calibration)
- **Internal ops (Felicia, Heather, Jen):** terse, directive, imperative. State the decision or the question, nothing more. No softening, no preamble.
- **Show partners / agents (Danny, Valli, Mark):** warmer leadership voice. Set expectations clearly, add a human closer ("Have a great weekend!"), mirror their phrasing when they're playful.
- **Vendors / support (WHOOP, Plaud):** functional and a little clipped. Answer or ask, hold position, don't volunteer information he's choosing to withhold. It's fine to answer a question with a question.
- **Cold email solicitations — the default: filter, don't reply.** Brett can't stand cold outreach. These get classified `Solicitation`, archived under `Solicitations`, and get **no draft** (per project rules). Do not reach for the warm-decline voice here.
- **Earned declines — the rare exception (Insperity model):** only when the sender did something to earn a human reply — showed up in person, followed up the right way, or is someone Brett might want a relationship with later (e.g. a future recruit). Signals it's *not* cold: a prior in-person touch, a genuine non-templated note, a name Brett recognizes or has researched. In those cases, the high-warmth no: upfront fast ("I want to be upfront so I don't waste your time"), close the door plainly ("we're not a fit, and that's not likely to change"), then give genuine, specific praise and leave a door open. **When unsure whether a sender earned this, treat it as a cold solicitation and filter** — flag it in the digest rather than drafting a warm reply on a guess.
- **Low-priority external replies you choose to answer (referrals, soft intros like Stretch Affect):** brief thanks + their name + the decision, then stop. e.g. "Thanks for the info, Sujeila. I'll reach out when I'm ready to schedule an assessment." Friendly but brief, not cold, not a single clipped fragment.
- **Peers / friendly vendors (Ryan):** banter allowed. Deflect compliments with humor, self-deprecate by proxy, keep it light.
- **Disputes / conflict (Yoss model):** controlled and evidence-based. Anchor every claim to the documented record, quote the other party's own words back, correct the facts, and refuse the bait. **Keep all venting out of the reply** — it goes to an internal thread, never the counterparty. Plain and careful, no emojis, no heat in the sent message.
- **Compliance / IRS / legal / tax:** plain, careful, minimal. No emojis, no ellipsis-casualness, no banter. (Carried from project rules; no sample in this pass — revisit when one appears.)
