# Branding Blueprint: website standard

_Shared reference. Source: Website StoryBrand Playbook (Drive 1ZFXHofcByaHvtevGLQq3wGUBTns5vNuyhXhVTyXLzVM), read in full 2026-11-02. StoryBrand SB7 itself is cited from content-frameworks.md (sections 3.1 and 12), not restated here. blueprint-audit reads the audit framework; blueprint-website-copy reads all of it. Built 2026-10-14; expanded 2026-11-02 with the blueprint-website-copy build (the 10/14 summary dropped parts of Playbook sections 6 to 9 and 11). Model of finished work: `examples/website-christa-foss.md`._

## Modes (Playbook section 2)

| Mode | Use | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Audit | Evaluate an existing homepage or service page | Evaluate what is visible; name what could not be read |
| Blueprint | Define what the site must communicate, its gaps, and what to emphasize | Lives in the Real Estate Branding Blueprint (blueprint-strategy); site work may land in Phase 1, 2, or 3 by impact and effort |
| Create or rewrite | Write homepage and service page copy | Gather the required inputs first; never assume missing positioning |

## Principles (Playbook section 3)

- The client is the hero; the agent is the guide.
- The problem is clear before the solution.
- Authority is demonstrated, not declared.
- Process reduces perceived client risk.
- Differentiation is concrete.
- Service plus geography is explicit.
- Copy is easy to understand and summarize.
- No em dashes, ever.

**Priorities (Playbook section 14):** clarity over creativity, specificity over cleverness, conversion over aesthetics. The goal is not a visually impressive site; it is a clear, credible, effective one.

## Required inputs (Playbook section 4)

**Audit:** homepage URL or copy, service page URLs or copy, market served, service focus, known proof points, business model. Missing: evaluate the visible messaging and note the limits.

**Create or rewrite (minimum):**

1. Market or markets served
2. Service focus (listings, buyers, balanced, luxury, relocation, investment, other)
3. Business model (solo, team lead, team-based, brokerage owner, other)
4. Primary audience
5. Differentiators
6. Proof points (experience, volume, transactions, reviews, certifications, media, specialization)
7. Risk reducer (access, structure, process, niche expertise)
8. Primary CTA
9. Rewrite or create from scratch
10. Tone or brand voice reference, if available

Materially incomplete: stop and ask (engagement). A test run uses labeled best guesses instead (blueprint-website-copy SKILL.md).

## Audit framework (Playbook section 5)

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| A. Hero clarity | Is the market clear? The service? The client problem? The outcome? Is the headline specific or generic? |
| B. Story structure | Is the client the hero and the agent the guide? Does the page move from problem to authority to action? |
| C. Authority | Is proof visible and early? Is credibility demonstrated or claimed? |
| D. Process | Is there a simple, believable process? Does it reduce risk? |
| E. Differentiation | Is there a real, concrete edge? Would it hold up side by side with competitors? |
| F. Conversion | Is there a clear next step? Is friction minimized? |
| G. Search and AI readiness | Is service plus geography explicit? Are headings clear? Is the page easy to summarize? Does it support recommendation-readiness? |

Audit output (section 9A): what is weak, vague, or missing; the clarity, authority, differentiation, and conversion gaps.

## Writing standard (blueprint-website-copy)

### Homepage (Playbook section 6)

| Section | Must do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| I. Hero | Clear market and service; the client problem; the outcome; primary CTA; optional secondary CTA | Slogans, vague branding, empty adjectives |
| II. Problem | Real client stakes: pricing uncertainty, negotiation pressure, timing stress, inventory constraints, transaction complexity, risk of costly mistakes. The client is the hero; tie it to real friction | Fear tactics, stakes the agent's market does not have |
| III. Authority | Proof: experience, volume, transactions, reviews, credentials, specialization, media presence | Vague superlatives, unsupported claims |
| IV. Process | A customized three-step structure from the agent's actual workflow that reinforces the differentiation and reduces risk | Generic steps ("clarity, strategy, execution") |
| V. Differentiation | Specific and defensible: marketing (exposure, media, targeting), negotiation (offer strategy, bidding management), data (pricing analysis, market modeling), network (off-market access, connections), systems (structured process, oversight) | Abstract positioning |
| VI. Risk reduction | Communication standards, oversight level, process clarity, timeline management | "Client-focused", "dedicated service", and their cousins |
| VII. Closing | One clear next step (schedule a consultation, request a pricing review, discuss your timeline) | Urgency manipulation |

The seven sections are the default, not a requirement (Brett, 2026-11-02). Use fewer, merge, or reorder when the page reads better for this agent (the example folds authority into the guide section); the hero and one clear next step are always there. Name any section left out, and why, in the return to Brett.

### Service pages (Playbook section 7)

One page per service focus the facts sheet confirms (Brett, 2026-11-02): for example selling in a named market, buying in a named market, luxury, relocation. Each page:

- pairs service plus geography explicitly (in the H1 and the SEO title)
- defines the audience
- defines the problem
- outlines the process
- reinforces the differentiation
- includes proof
- ends with a CTA matched to the page's intent (a seller page asks for a pricing review, a buyer page for a search consultation)

Service pages are more specific than the homepage. A page of downloads is not a service page.

### SEO title and meta description (Brett, 2026-11-02)

One of each per page. Title: service plus geography first, then the agent or team name, 60 characters or fewer. Meta description: the page's promise and next step in plain words, 155 characters or fewer, no keyword stuffing. Counted by script.

### Structure for search and AI (Playbook section 8)

Clear headings; explicit market references; service plus geography repeated naturally; low ambiguity; easy to summarize; supports recommendation-style search. FAQ sections may be used when helpful. Never reference LLM or search mechanics in the copy.

### Rewrite and create output (Playbook section 9)

Rewrite: follow the homepage or service page structure, correct every weakness the audit named, align with this standard. Create from scratch: confirm the inputs, then write structured copy.

### Consistency (Playbook section 11)

The site aligns with the portal bios, Google Business Profile, social profiles, and the overall positioning. Inconsistency weakens clarity and recommendation-readiness. When `bios.md` exists, the site states the same facts with the same values.

### Tone (Playbook section 12)

Professional, clear, confident by default; adjusted to the brand voice, market tier, and audience (luxury understated, high-volume teams structured and decisive, community-driven warmer). No hype, clichés, or exaggerated claims.

### Data integrity (Playbook section 13)

Never fabricate proof, results, or differentiators. Ask when missing (integrity.md).

## Checklist

Hero names market, service, problem, outcome, and CTA, with no slogan; any of the seven sections left out is named with the reason; the client is the hero and the agent the guide; the problem comes before the solution; proof is early and demonstrated; the three steps are the agent's own; differentiation is defensible; risk reducers are specific and true; one clear CTA per page, repeated, no urgency manipulation; one service page per confirmed service focus, each more specific than the homepage; service plus geography in every H1 and SEO title; titles and metas within length; consistent with the portals, Google Business Profile, and social; nothing fabricated; no LLM mechanics in the copy; no em dashes.

# Example: homepage, Christa Foss

_Model of finished work for blueprint-website-copy (Brett, 2026-11-02). Source: https://www.christafoss.com/ (homepage), read 2026-11-02 in the built-in browser. A section-by-section breakdown with short excerpts, not a copy of the page. Read it for structure, warmth, and pacing; never reuse its facts, phrases, or headlines for another agent. Where it and website-standard.md disagree, the standard wins (the overrides below). Her seller page is a page of downloads, not a service page, so it is not a model for service pages._

## The page, in order

| # | Section | What it does | Standard section it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | A warm line, "When you're ready to buy or sell, I'm your realtor girl", a one-sentence promise of support, one button ("Connect with Christa") | I. Hero (partly: see override 1) |
| 2 | Problem | Opens "Home buying & selling doesn't have to be stressful", then four questions the client asks at night: is now the right time, how do rates affect me, will I overpay or undervalue, does my agent have my best interest in mind. Ends on the stakes: the right agent is the difference between a smooth and a bumpy ride | II. Problem |
| 3 | Services | Three short blocks (buying, selling, staging), two sentences each, then the button again | Bridge to the service pages |
| 4 | Testimonial | A headline pulled from the review ("You definitely need Christa in your corner!"), the quote, the client type in brackets ("First-time buyers") | III. Authority (social proof) |
| 5 | Guide | Empathy first ("I know how personal home is to you"), then one human detail that explains how she works (hospitality background from retail, guest relations, ministry), then her style in plain words ("equal parts grit and grace") and a communication promise ("You'll never be in the dark") | Guide, VI. Risk reduction |
| 6 | Track record | A scrolling strip of 36 recent sales and purchases labeled Sold or Purchased | III. Authority (demonstrated) |
| 7 | Awards | Specific, dated awards with what each means ("comprised of the top 20% of agents in each office"), plus her coaching role | III. Authority |
| 8 | Two testimonials | One from a mortgage partner, one from sellers with a concrete outcome (sold in three days, multiple offers over asking) | III. Authority, V. Differentiation |
| 9 | Plan | "Here's how to get the ball rolling": three numbered steps, with "no pressure, just a conversation" | IV. Process |
| 10 | Testimonial | Buyers and sellers on how she walked beside them | Success |
| 11 | Close | "Ready to make your move? Let's talk, coffee's on me!" and the button | VII. Closing |

## What to take from it

- **The problem is the client's own voice.** Four questions the reader has actually asked themselves do more than any statement about the market.
- **Empathy before proof.** The guide section starts with how the client feels and only then turns to her background.
- **The human detail explains the method.** Hospitality is why she runs a welcoming, communicative process, not a bio fact.
- **Proof is shown, not claimed.** A strip of real transactions, dated awards with their meaning, and testimonials tagged by client type.
- **Testimonials do structural work.** Each is placed between sections and carries its own headline drawn from the quote.
- **One CTA, repeated.** The same button appears after every major section, so the reader never has to look for it.
- **Low-pressure close.** The next step is small and human.
- **Short blocks.** No paragraph runs past four lines on screen; nothing is stacked or listed except the three steps.

## Overrides (website-standard.md wins)

1. **Hero.** Hers is a slogan ("There's no place like home") and never names her market or service in the hero; the market appears only in the page title (South Metro Minnesota). Our hero names market, service, the client's problem, and the outcome above the button.
2. **Process.** Her three steps (schedule time, start exploring, make your move) are generic and could belong to any agent. Ours come from the agent's actual workflow and carry the differentiation.
3. **Differentiation.** She has no section that would hold up side by side with a competitor; it lives in testimonials only. Ours names one defensible edge from the five categories.
4. **Search and AI readiness.** Service plus geography appears in the title tag only. Ours repeats it naturally in headings and body.
5. **Personal branding lines.** "Realtor girl" fits her brand voice; a line like it is used only when the client's voice reference supports it.
