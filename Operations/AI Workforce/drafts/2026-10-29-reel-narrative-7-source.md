## Transcript (verbatim, as transcribed)

You need to learn how to lead a volunteer army. I got into direct sales after college and I'm bringing in more orders, but I'm not making as much money as some of the other people. Why is that? Because they had teams. So I'm like, this is easy. I'll go out and I'll just recruit other agents to come in and since she worked for me. And I failed miserably at it. So I didn't understand the disconnect. The great professional athletes, they've got the most talent and the most work ethic, and they get frustrated with their teammates because they just don't understand. How come you don't work as hard as I am? How come you can't get the same results? And when I realized that that was my deficiency, OK, I don't have the skill yet to lead, mentor, train other people. And that was my pivot point, but it still took me a long time to develop those skills.

(Note: "and since she worked for me" is a likely mistranscription; the meaning is "and have them work for me".)

## Brett's interview answers (2026-10-29, verbatim)

**Q1. What did you sell in direct sales, and what did failing at that first team look like?**
Direct sales were telecommunications products. It was easy to recruit sales reps because I could paint the big picture of what was possible. Then tried to push them to work as hard as I did, get up early, work late, don't take rejection personally and just keep grinding. They were independent reps working commission-only, so it was easy to slack off or quit. that's why I referred to them as a volunteer army.

**Q2. What was the first thing you changed about how you lead once you saw the gap?**
Stop trying to change other people's behaviors

**Q3. When a top producer tells you they're about to hire a team, what do you see them get wrong first?**
They hire wrong people who are typically inexperienced, needy and require too much time and handholding.

**Follow-up (2026-10-29). Once you stopped trying to change people's behaviors, what did you do instead?**
Hired differently (Brett picked this option: selecting people who already behave the way he needs, which ties to narrative 1).

**Brett, 2026-10-29 (correction):** "You need to learn how to lead a volunteer army" was his mentor's line to him, a lesson he learned. "Once I stopped acting like a boss and more like a leader, everything changed."
