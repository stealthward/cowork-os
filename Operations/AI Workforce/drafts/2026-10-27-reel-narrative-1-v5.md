# Reel narrative 1, v5: Factory Ep 1 R4, three legs of the stool

_Copywriter draft per Brett's 2026-10-28 notes (business model in his words, "I" for his own experience, tighter hook) plus the editor's two open v4 fixes. I spent years and several companies trying to turn a point guard into a center. Stable, long-term growth came when we started finding and keeping A players, and this is how we've done it.

Think of a stool. It only stands if every leg holds, and if one goes, the whole thing tumbles over. For us, the three legs are talent acquisition, business model, and culture.

**Leg one is talent acquisition.** We look for A players, people who come wired with their own personal standards and bring their own hustle and work ethic.

That work ethic comes from within and can't be trained, and it took me a long time to accept it. Now we put our effort into finding people who already have it, and into building a group whose standards line up.

**Leg two is the business model.** An A player needs an opportunity big enough to reach their goals and achieve their dreams, and big enough to keep them motivated. To make it big enough, we have to understand what their life and career goals are and see whether we're a mutual fit.

Our model has to let someone reach their goals financially, in their lifestyle, and in their work satisfaction. If we aren't aligned in both the short and long term, we'll part ways at some point.

**Leg three is culture, and it's how we keep them.** A players don't want to hang out with C players, so when the culture is right the team largely sorts itself out.

I held on to underperformers for way too long, and it came from a place of scarcity, like keeping someone because they bring in revenue. Letting them go has helped the organization every time.

We also build trust on purpose: we're open with people about where they stand, we let them miss and learn, and our leaders own their mistakes.

**The bottom line takes care of itself if you grow your people.**
