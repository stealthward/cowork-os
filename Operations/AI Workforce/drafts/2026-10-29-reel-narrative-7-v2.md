Building a team can make your production plummet.
Leading is a different job, and I learned that by failing first.

A team is a volunteer army. People choose whether to follow you, and leading them is a skill of its own.

I found that out in direct sales after college [BRETT: what the company sold]. I was bringing in more orders, but I wasn't making as much money as some of the other people, because they had teams. So I decided to recruit other agents [BRETT: how many] to work for me. I failed miserably.

I was like the talented athlete who wonders why teammates can't get the same results. It took me a while to see the real gap: I didn't have the skill yet to lead, mentor, and train other people. Seeing that was my pivot point, and building those skills still took a long time.

I've seen the same thing from the other side. With 10,000 plus transactions closed through my escrow company, I've got a frame of reference for what works and what doesn't. I've had clients who are massive producers say, "I'll hire a bunch of people and it'll be easy. I'll just show them how to do what I know how to do." Then their production plummets and they feel like they're herding cats.

They're stuck on the question I was stuck on: why can't they just do what I do? Moving from salesperson to sales manager is a totally different persona and a completely different role.

So my first questions are about the system: what's your onboarding process, and what's your training process? I had to build my own answers to both [BRETT: what onboarding and training you built, and how long it took].

**Leading people is a skill of its own, and you can learn it.**
