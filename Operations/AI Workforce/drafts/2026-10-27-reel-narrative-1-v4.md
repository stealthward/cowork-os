If you want long-term, stable growth, you need to find and keep A players. This is how we've done it.

Think of a stool. It only stands if every leg holds, and if one goes, the whole thing tumbles over. For us, the three legs are talent acquisition, business model, and culture.

**Leg one is talent acquisition.** We look for A players, people who come wired with their own personal standards and bring their own hustle and work ethic.

That work ethic comes from within and can't be trained, and we spent years and several companies trying to turn a point guard into a center before we learned it. These days we put our effort into finding people who already have it, and into building a group whose standards line up.

**Leg two is the business model.** Our A players need an opportunity big enough to reach their goals and achieve their dreams, and big enough to keep them motivated.

[BRETT: how we make the opportunity big enough, one or two sentences]

**Leg three is culture, and it's how we keep them.** A players don't want to hang out with C players, so when the culture is right the team largely sorts itself out. Holding on to underperformers always came from scarcity for us, and letting them go has helped the organization every time.

We also build trust on purpose: we're open with people about where they stand, we let them miss and learn, and our leaders own their mistakes first.

**The bottom line takes care of itself if you grow your people.**
