Eval: blueprint-portal-bio

What good looks like before a set of portal bios reaches Brett.

Owner: Brett Ward Used by: Brett, on the Jim McInerney test and every run until the skill passes; graded against the objectives below, never against a past output Version: 1.0, 2026-10-30 (built from the Portal Optimization Playbook checklist in .claude/blueprint/reference/portal-standard.md, integrity.md, and the approved scope) Pass bar: weighted score of 4.0 or higher, no criterion below 3, zero auto-fails Test runs save to: Operations/AI Workforce/evals/blueprint-portal-bio/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<client>/

How to score

Score each criterion 1 to 5. Reasoning first, then the number. If bio-facts.md is missing or cites no sources, mark "cannot assess" and send it back.

Who scores: criterion 1 is scored by the CMO from the fact-checker's result; Brett scores criteria 2 to 7. The CMO asks them in question boxes (AskUserQuestion), one criterion per question, in order: each states the CMO's score and reasoning and offers "Agree" first, then other scores. Then one box for the overall rating (useful, partly, noise). The CMO records the answers here; Brett never opens this file to score.

Criteria

1. Every fact sourced (weight 3) A 5: every fact in every bio traces to a bio-facts.md row marked Confirmed, or to a Portal-computed row stated as a range or floor; the fact-checker's re-run is clean. A 1: any fact with no row, a client claim or conflicted figure used unconfirmed, or a Portal-computed figure stated exactly without approval.

2. Opening and positioning (weight 3) A 5: sentence 1 states market, role, and transaction focus; sentence 2 a concrete differentiator with proof; sentence 3 the risk reducer or service model; the strongest proof sits early; it reads as prose, not a checklist; it agrees with the Blueprint's positioning when one exists. A 1: a generic opener ("Known for providing the highest degree of service") or the market and focus buried.

3. Specific, not generic (weight 2) A 5: the differentiator is concrete and from one of the five categories (marketing, negotiation, data, network, process); the risk reducer is specific and true; none of the banned phrases ("client-focused", "dedicated to service", "committed to excellence") or their cousins. A 1: copy that could describe any agent in the market.

4. Business model accurate (weight 2) A 5: solo or team wording matches the profile type on each portal; nothing assumes seller-first, hyperlocal, solo, team, a backstory, or media presence the facts sheet does not support; team members named only from a current, confirmed roster. A 1: a team profile written as a solo agent, or a stale roster repeated.

5. Cross-portal variance (weight 2) A 5: every version states the same positioning, proof, and service model with the same values; phrasing, order, rhythm, and closing line differ enough that no two read as copies. A 1: near-identical versions, or a fact that changes between versions.

6. Audit useful (weight 1) A 5: the audit names what is weak, generic, inconsistent, or buried on each portal, the contradictions across portals, and what to fix first, each citing portals.md; the facts the client must confirm are listed. A 1: no audit, or findings without citations.

7. Ready to post (weight 1) A 5: Brett would send these to the client after a light pass; first person (or as briefed); within 175 to 200 words or the portal's verified field limit, counted by script; tone fits the band (luxury understated, team structured, community warmer). A 1: Brett would rewrite.

Automatic fails

  • Any fabricated fact: a figure, credential, award, review, roster, or outcome not in a source
  • A fact that differs between two portal versions
  • A conflict resolved by picking a value instead of asking
  • A core input (market, transaction focus, business model, differentiator, risk reducer) filled by inference instead of the facts gate
  • Brett's voice or Brett's facts in a client's bio
  • Any portal updated, anything sent, or anyone contacted
  • An em dash in any bio
