### Ep 1 R3 (Factory - EP1 - R3.mp4): Talent: you can't train work ethic; find, don't make

That innate hustle, that work ethic, that comes from within. What I now realize is that you can't train them to do that. And so I shifted my focus. And again, this comes after years and several companies of, I always call it, trying to turn a point guard into a center. Fundamentally, you're not going to be able to make that change. Just because I see that somebody has got a core competency, I'm not going to be the one that's going to make them want to go out and do it. And it shifted from trying to make somebody into trying to find somebody. And that, for me, is how I've evolved in wanting to not be a solopreneur, eat what I kill, versus I want to grow an organization.

### Ep 1 R5 (Factory - EP1 - R5.mp4): Talent and culture: letting underperformers go; A players and C players

Every time I finally got to the point where I let them go, everybody else was like, oh my god, thank you, what took so long? I want people that are gonna be motivated to provide for the team as well as themselves. Because the opposite of that is true. If you have somebody that comes in and they're only motivated out of self-interest, they're gonna wean themselves out anyway. But you can kinda see that right away. You really can. And if you've got the right culture, it starts to self-manage. Because A players don't wanna hang out with C players. And they become isolated real fast. And it's interesting when you get to that point, because I had a lot of D and F players at certain times. And you go through these growing pains, and then you start to recognize the patterns. And I used to hold on to them for way too long. Well, this one brings in revenue, and this one does this, and this one does this. It comes from a place of scarcity. And every time I've had to make those hard decisions, what ultimately has happened is it's been a net positive for the organization.

### Ep 2 R9 (Factory - EP2 - R9.mp4): Culture: how we build a culture of trust

We encourage failure. For us, building a culture of trust intentionally is being completely transparent. On an individual level, people want to know where they stand. People can take and understand the constructive feedback as long as they know that they're in a safe environment. And then all the way back to the leadership, it's owning your mistakes, being a little bit vulnerable, and to be able to provide a platform and say, OK, well, Here's where you are, here's your path, we're gonna help you get there. It's okay if you make mistakes along the way, as long as you learn from them and continue to improve. And by having that interaction on a regular and ongoing basis, that in and of itself builds, because they know that we're there for their best interest. It's not all about our bottom line. The bottom line takes care of itself if you grow your people.
