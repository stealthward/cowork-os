## Research brief (as returned, condensed)

**Headline.** 220-character limit; about 60 to 70 characters show in search results, comments, and connection requests (about 65 is the working limit). Practitioners say headline keywords carry heavy weight in LinkedIn search (not confirmed by LinkedIn). LinkedIn's own Help says to use it to promote an area of expertise. Common formats: role, audience, and proof; or "helping [audience] achieve [result]".
Sources: authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit (undated); eden.so/blog/linkedin-character-limits (2026-08-21); linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-headline-formulas (2026); linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351 (undated).

**About.** 2,600-character limit. Before "see more": about 220 to 300 characters on desktop, often under 150 on mobile (sources disagree; LinkedIn does not publish the cutoffs). First person, with the first line able to stand alone; who you help, background, proof with numbers, then a closing line. LinkedIn Help: one or two paragraphs expressing mission, motivation, and skills.
Sources: eden.so (2026-08-21); lilachbullock.com/how-long-should-linkedin-profile-summary-be (undated); authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-about-section (2026-04-23, updated 2026-06-08); linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351.

**Authority features.** Creator Mode was retired as a toggle in March 2024; its tools are now on by default (practitioner sources only). Follow can be set as the primary profile button (LinkedIn Help a762822, about 2024). Profile topic hashtags were removed in early 2024 (not confirmed by LinkedIn). Featured pins content; a custom URL helps people find you (LinkedIn Help topic a64). No source on banner images.

**Could not verify:** exact truncation lengths and limits on a LinkedIn-owned page; the Creator Mode retirement announcement; banner effect. Only two LinkedIn-owned pages had substance; the practitioner sites cite each other.
