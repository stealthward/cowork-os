If you want long-term, stable growth, you need to find and keep A players. This is how we've done it.

Think of a stool. It only stands if every leg holds, and if one goes, the whole thing tumbles over. For us, the three legs are talent acquisition, business model, and culture.

**Leg one is talent acquisition, and the lesson is to find them, not make them.** That hustle and work ethic comes from within, and you can't train it.

We spent years and several companies trying to turn a point guard into a center before we learned that. So we shifted from trying to make somebody into trying to find somebody, a group of people who are aligned and come wired with their own personal standards.

**Leg two is the business model.** An A player needs an opportunity big enough to reach their goals and achieve their dreams, and big enough to stay motivated. [BRETT: how we make the opportunity big enough, one or two sentences]

**Leg three is culture, and it's how we keep them.** A players don't want to hang out with C players, so with the right culture the team starts to self-manage and the C players become isolated fast.

Letting people go is where we used to get it wrong. We held on for way too long, telling ourselves this one brings in revenue and that one does this, and that comes from a place of scarcity.

Every time we finally made the hard call, the rest of the team said thank you and asked what took so long. It has been a net positive for the organization every time.

We build trust on purpose, starting with being completely transparent, because people want to know where they stand. They can take constructive feedback as long as they know they're in a safe environment.

We encourage failure, and our leaders own their mistakes. It's okay to miss as long as we learn from it, and we show people their path and help them get there.

**The bottom line takes care of itself if you grow your people.**
