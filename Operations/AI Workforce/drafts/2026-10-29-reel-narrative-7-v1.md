Building a team can make your production plummet.
Leading is a different job, and I learned that by failing first.

Think about a great professional athlete. They have the most talent and put in the most work, and they get frustrated with their teammates because they just don't understand. How come you don't work as hard as I do? How come you can't get the same results?

Great producers do the same thing with a team. I know, because I did it first.

**Assumption.** I got into direct sales after college [BRETT: what the company sold]. I was bringing in more orders, but I wasn't making as much money as some of the other people. The reason was simple: they had teams.

So I thought, this is easy. I'll recruit other agents [BRETT: how many] to come in and work for me. I failed miserably at it.

**Gap.** I didn't understand the disconnect. When I finally did, it was my deficiency: I didn't have the skill yet to lead, mentor, and train other people.

**Pivot.** Seeing that was my pivot point. It still took me a long time to develop those skills [BRETT: how long, and what onboarding or training you built]. Leading a team is closer to leading a volunteer army, because people choose whether to follow, and that takes a skill of its own.

**Pattern.** With 10,000 plus transactions closed through my escrow company, I have a frame of reference for what works and what doesn't. I've had clients who are massive producers say, "I'll hire a bunch of people and it'll be easy. I'll just show them how to do what I know how to do." Then their production plummets and they feel like they're herding cats. They never reconcile why people can't just do what they do.

Moving from salesperson to sales manager is a totally different persona and a completely different role. So when a producer tells me this story, my first questions are: what's your onboarding process, and what's your training process?

**Leading people is a skill of its own, and you can learn it.**
