## Part A. Load now

### A1. The Content Unit: Hook, Retain, Reward (L-FC, Part I, "The Content Unit - Three Components")
A content unit is the smallest piece that hooks, retains, and rewards attention. All audience-growing content rewards the person consuming it, which only happens if they have a reason to consume it, pay attention long enough, and get that reason satisfied.
1. **Hook attention:** get them to notice it. Measured by the percentage of people who start consuming it. Three levers: topics, headlines, format (A2 to A4).
2. **Retain attention:** get them to consume it (A5).
3. **Reward attention:** satisfy the reason they started (A6).
- Editor checks: hook, retention device, and payoff are all present; the payoff is the one the hook promised.

### A2. Hook lever 1: Topics (L-FC, Part I, "Topics")
Personal experience first: no one else has lived Brett's life, so no one can say it his way.
1. **Far Past:** important past lessons, tied to what he does now ("the story without the scar").
2. **Recent Past:** something he did, then what happened. Mine last week's calendar, meetings, and conversations. Client stories and testimonials live here.
3. **Present:** capture ideas the moment they come; post them; if one beats normal, make more on that topic.
4. **Trending:** go where attention is and apply his experience to it. Widest reach.
5. **Manufactured:** pick a topic, learn it or do it on purpose, then show it. Most effort, biggest possible payoff.
- Editor checks: the topic is something only Brett could say; its category is named in the brief.
6. **Other people's experience** (kept from content-frameworks.md "Draw from Experience"): stories from clients, guests, partners, or the market.
- Brett, 2026-10-05: the copywriter brief uses these six; they replace "Draw from Experience."

### A3. Hook lever 2: Headlines (L-FC, Part I, "Headlines")
A headline gives the reason to consume and lets the viewer weigh the likely reward against everything else. Seven components, from a news-headline meta-analysis. Use at least two.
1. **Recency:** as recent as possible.
2. **Relevancy:** personally meaningful to the viewer.
3. **Celebrity:** prominent people or authorities.
4. **Proximity:** close to home geographically.
5. **Conflict:** opposing ideas, people, or forces.
6. **Unusual:** odd, unique, rare, bizarre.
7. **Ongoing:** a story still unfolding, with updates or twists.
- Add from CW §1: headline principles are curiosity, differentiation, and appeal; a headline is new wrapping on the same offer; the first three seconds count visually and verbally; tested formula: "How to [Yay] without [Boo], even if..."
- Editor checks: at least two of the seven; the body pays off what the headline promises.
- Brett, 2026-10-05: all seven load into content-frameworks.md, replacing the four there; the editor checks for at least two per headline. (His Hook Retain Reward Doc lists five; Unusual and Ongoing were missing.)

### A4. Hook lever 3: Format (L-FC, Part I, "Format")
People consume what looks like things they already liked.
1. Format for the platform first.
2. Tweak for the ideal audience.
3. Use the best-performing content in his market on that platform as the model.
- Editor checks: looks native to the platform; a benchmark piece was named in the brief.

### A5. Retain: Lists, Steps, Stories (L-FC, Part I, "Retain")
Curiosity is the main driver. Plant an unanswered question, stated or implied.
1. **Lists:** items on a theme in any order. State the number in the headline or first seconds.
2. **Steps:** actions in a required order toward a result. Clear, valuable early steps make people want the rest.
3. **Stories:** real or imagined events, usually with a lesson; they run on "what happens next."
- Steps versus lists: steps are less flexible with a clearer reward; lists are flexible with a less explicit reward. All three can be combined (a story per list item, a list inside a step).
- Editor checks: an open question is planted and closed; anything labeled "steps" is truly sequential.

### A6. Reward: value per second (L-FC, Part I, "Reward")
Quality is how often the piece rewards the viewer for the time it takes. Nothing is too long, only too boring.
1. Hook the right audience (topic, headline, format).
2. Retain with lists, steps, stories.
3. Fully satisfy the reason they started.
- Failure modes: delivering fewer items than promised, weak or already-heard items, or the wrong audience.
- The audience decides if it is good. The test is whether the audience grows.
- Editor checks: every promised item is delivered; every question opened is answered.

### A7. Short versus long form (L-FC, Part I, closing passage)
Long content is content units linked together; each section needs its own hook and payoff. Start short, then build.
- Editor checks: no stretch of a long piece goes without a payoff.

### A8. Give : Ask ratio, "Give until they ask" (L-FC, Part II, "Mastering the Give : Ask ratio")
Content deposits goodwill; asks withdraw it.
- Mature platforms show the minimum sustainable ratio: about 3.5 to 1 (TV) and 4 to 1 (Facebook). Growing platforms give far more than they ask, so model those.
- The author's rule: "Give until they ask." Give in public, ask in private; the people who reach out self-select and are the best customers.
- Asking for money is the moment you choose to slow growth.
- Fit for Brett: this is the mechanism behind the give-first rule in growth-strategy.md. It applies to his personal content now; the ask mechanics (A-lane items in Part B) wait for phase 2.
- Editor checks: a value piece carries no ask; any batch stays far above 4 to 1.

### A9. 7 Lessons from making content (L-FC, Part II, "7 Lessons I've Learned From Making Content")
1. **Switch from "How to" to "How I."** Talk about what he did and what he prefers, not what others should do. Experience cannot be argued with and does not sound preachy.
2. **We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught.** Most of the audience misses most posts; repeat core points. He will tire of a message long before they have all seen it.
3. **Puddles, Ponds, Lakes, Oceans.** Start with a narrow niche plus place, then widen.
4. **Content creates tools for salespeople.** Keep a "greatest hits" list, each tagged with the problem it solves and the benefit; send them before or after conversations.
5. **Free content retains paying customers.** Good free content makes clients stay longer; bad free content lowers their view of the paid work.
6. **People don't have shorter attention spans, they have higher standards.**
7. ~~Avoid pre-scheduling posts.~~ Left out (Brett, 2026-10-05): scheduled publishing stays the default.
- Editor checks: written in "How I," not "you should"; scoped to Brett's niche.

### A10. Benchmarks (L-FC, Part II, "Benchmarks")
Monthly: (1) total followers and reach (size); (2) rate of growth, new divided by starting (speed), absolute and relative.
- Only inputs are controllable: fix a posting cadence and an ask cadence per platform, then do not stop. Results only mean something when inputs are consistent.
- Use: the CMO's monthly report on initiative 4.

### A11. Scaling: Depth then width, or Width then depth (L-FC, Part II, "How to Scale It")
- **Depth then width:** (1) post on one relevant platform; (2) post regularly; (3) maximize quality and quantity there; (4) add a platform while holding the first; (5) repeat. Uses fewer resources and compounds; relies on one channel early.
- **Width then depth:** (1) post on one platform; (2) post regularly; (3) add the next while holding the last; (4) continue until on all relevant platforms; (5) maximize all together. Reaches a broader audience faster and allows repurposing; takes more labor.
- Use: the Q4 personal content plan names which one it follows.

### A12. Copywriting rules for phase 1 content (CW §2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 12, Bonus)
1. **Say what only you can say:** unique, verifiable proof over vague promises; competitors can copy an offer, not his proof or story.
2. **Call out who it is for, and who it is not:** be specific, match the reader's awareness level, and describe real moments of frustration.
3. **Always have a reason why:** "because" raises compliance; each line sells the next line.
4. **Damaging admissions:** admit the flaw first; "[damaging truth] BUT [what you want them to believe]" builds believability.
5. **Show, don't tell:** describe the moment, not the benefit.
6. **Implied authority:** let experience, clientele, and volume answer "why believe you" without claiming it outright.
7. **Third grade reading level:** simple beats clever; assume a distracted reader.
8. **Humor (bonus):** a differentiator, not a crutch; test it.
- Editor checks: one line on each, folded into the existing voice checklist (most already overlap voice-principles.md).
