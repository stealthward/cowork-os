## 13. LinkedIn playbook (Brett's own page)

This section is the one place the copywriter, editor, and CMO look to see how a LinkedIn piece is built. It holds almost no rules of its own: each row points to the section where the rule lives. New rules appear only where a rule did not exist, and each is marked "new". It covers Brett's own page in phase 1, so every piece is give-first (growth-strategy.md; 3.6). Company pages (RTM, Five Star) are outside it.

Rulings that govern every row (Brett, 2026-10-29; the ACQ.ai LinkedIn brief of 2026-10-29 is the source of the formats, and these rulings override it where it differs):
- No call to action on Brett's own posts (3.6; 4.3a item 10; 9 item 4).
- No hashtags (voice-principles.md, Emojis and hashtags; 9 item 3).
- No Labels as hook callouts (4.3a item 10).
- No em dashes (voice-principles.md, Words and Phrases to Avoid).

### 13.4 Repurposing: one idea, three appearances

Source: section 7 item 10 and the ACQ.ai LinkedIn brief ("change the format, not the message"). The sequence is a reel caption first, then a story post, then a short text or list.

| Step | Appearance | What changes | What stays |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reel caption (13.1) | Posted with the finished reel. Adds one or two beats the clip does not say. | The idea |
| 2 | Story post (13.1) | The full story from the interview, no video, new hook pattern (13.2 item 1) | The idea and the lesson |
| 3 | Short text or list (13.1a) | The lesson alone, with its reason | The idea and the lesson |

Rules:
1. Each appearance is a new piece in the section 2 map and gets its own hook, edit, and fact-check. Individual sentences may repeat; a whole paragraph tracking the earlier appearance is a fix (4.4, applied to Brett's own earlier post).
2. An appearance never retells the one before it (section 2 reel row).
3. A story post is written only when the interview yields a full story. No story means no step 2 for that idea.
4. The message does not change. If the later format needs a different claim, it is a second idea and a second post (7 item 10).
5. Each idea has one record: the reel it came from, and the dates Brett actually posted each appearance. Agents read the dates and never set them.

**Spacing between appearances of the same concept: CMO proposal, unsourced; Brett to set.** No file gives a spacing figure. Proposal:
- At least 14 days between one appearance and the next.
- No two appearances of one concept in the same calendar week.
- All three appearances inside 8 weeks of the first.

Reasoning: a gap of two weeks gives the first post time to show how it landed before the next format is written, which follows the "learn from what lands" loop in 3.5 and the 70/20/10 logic in 4.3a item 17. It also keeps the second appearance from reading as a repost. Section 8 item 2 says people need to be reminded more than taught, which argues against waiting long, and the 8-week window keeps the three appearances reading as one topic. The 14 days and 8 weeks are judgment, not measurement.

**Cadence tiers** (pieces per week; weeks = 27 eligible reels divided by reel captions per week):

| Tier | Reel captions | Story posts | Short posts | Pieces per week | Share that is video | Weeks the 27 reels last | Weeks if all 99 qualify | Ideas needed for stories and shorts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Steady | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 100% | 13.5 (27 / 2) | 49.5 (99 / 2) | none |
| B: Build | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 50% | 13.5 (27 / 2) | 49.5 (99 / 2) | 13.5 stories (50% of the 27 ideas) and 13.5 shorts (50%) |
| C: Volume | 3 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 43% | 9.0 (27 / 3) | 33.0 (99 / 3) | 9 stories (33%) and 27 shorts (100%) |

Reading the math:
- Stories and shorts reuse ideas the reels already supplied (13.4), so they do not use up reels. Reel supply runs out at the reel-caption rate alone.
- With the 14-day spacing proposal (13.4), the last idea's third appearance lands 4 weeks after its caption, so Tiers B and C run about 4 weeks past the figures above (about 17.5 and 13.0 weeks). Tier A has no tail.
- Each idea can appear at most three times (caption, story, short), so the 27 ideas cap at 81 appearances. Tier B uses 54 (4 x 13.5), Tier C uses 63 (7 x 9). Tier C therefore needs every idea to run as a short, which leaves no room to skip a weak one.
- The story slot depends on interviews that yield full stories. How many of the 27 will is unknown. In any week without a ready story, skip the slot; do not fill it with a weaker piece (3.5: quality earns attention).
- Final passes for Brett per week: 2, 4, and 7.
- Against the measured rates: Tier A is above Ryan Serhant (1.1) and below the other nine authors (7.0 to 63.6). Tier C matches Justin Welsh (7.0) and Codie Sanchez (7.4). Tier B video share (50%) and Tier C (43%) sit inside the measured range of 0% to 61%.

**Recommendation: Tier B** [CMO recommendation; Brett to confirm, then delete this bracket].
- Pros: the reel supply lasts as long as under Tier A (13.5 weeks), because the two extra pieces reuse ideas; it puts the repurposing rule (7 item 10) to work; it gives story posts a regular home; two pieces a week from Brett's own reels matches the cadence he set on 2026-10-26.
- Cons: it doubles Brett's final passes and needs interview time for each story; the story supply is unknown; no source shows that more posts per week grows the audience.
- Why not A: it leaves the longer interview answers unused. Why not C: it exhausts the reel supply in 9 weeks and needs every idea to be a short.

### 13.7 What we measure

Measurement follows 4.3a item 17, which applies once a lane has performance data of its own. A winner is judged against Brett's own account, never an invented threshold, across three tiers: attention, consumption, and business. An attention winner with no business result is not a winner. Each batch follows 70% proven winners, 20% permutations, and 10% new. Monthly follower count and growth rate follow 10.1.

New (CMO proposal): each posted piece is logged against its idea record (13.4) with the format, pattern, awareness level, meat, word count, and the date Brett posted it, so item 17 can compare like with like. Which LinkedIn metrics fill the attention, consumption, and business tiers is not defined in any source; Brett decides (Part 3, decision 7).
