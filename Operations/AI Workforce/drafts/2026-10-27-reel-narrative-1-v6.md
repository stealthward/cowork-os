# Reel narrative 1, v6: Factory Ep 1 R4, three legs of the stool

_Copywriter draft from Brett's line feedback on v5 (2026-10-28): new hook, his stool paragraph and Talent section verbatim, legs in the order talent, culture, model, culture and model in his voice. LinkedIn, Brett's page, text post with the reel attached as native video._

---

Stable, long-term growth comes from finding A players and keeping them, and it took me years to learn how. This is what we do now.

I look at our company like a three-legged stool. If one of them is weak, the stool collapses. For us, those three legs are: talent, culture, and model.

**Talent.** We only hire A players. These are people who come wired with their own personal standards. They take pride in everything they do because of what it means to them, not someone else. It's something you can't teach.

The same is true of work ethic. These are not traits that can be trained, and it took my ego a long time to accept that.

I've worked with countless people who had the potential, and I thought I was good enough to help them reach it. I might get a spike in performance for a couple of weeks or even a few months, but they always return to their baseline.

Instead of trying to build people into something they're not, we now put our efforts into finding people who already have it.

**Culture.** This is how we keep them. A players don't want to hang out with C players, so when the culture is right, the team largely sorts itself out.

I held on to underperformers for way too long. It came from a place of scarcity, like keeping someone because they bring in revenue. Letting them go has helped the organization every time.

We build trust on purpose. We're open with people about where they stand, we let them miss and learn, and our leaders own their mistakes.

**Model.** An A player needs an opportunity big enough to reach their goals and stay motivated. To make it big enough, we have to understand their life and career goals and see whether we're a mutual fit.

Our model has to let someone reach their goals financially, in their lifestyle, and in their work satisfaction. If we aren't aligned in both the short and long term, we'll part ways at some point.

When the fit is right, the person grows and the company grows with them.

**The bottom line takes care of itself if you grow your people.**
