## How to use this file

Keep three posts here, no more. Each entry is the full post text plus one line on why it worked: the hook, the format, the payoff. Use these examples to match execution, not to police topic selection. If a new draft shares a topic with one of these posts, keep the topic and borrow the winning hook shape, voice, structure, and close. When a new post beats one of these, swap out the weakest. Three is enough for Cowork to pattern-match against; more dilutes the signal. To find your real winners, paste your LinkedIn analytics and ask Cowork to identify the posts with the strongest performance signals.

## Example 1 — Career Advice

Referred candidates are 3-4x more likely to be hired.

At #Google that gap is even larger.

❌ But here's what NOT to do:

- Message a stranger on LinkedIn
- Ask for a referral in your first conversation
- Send generic "can you refer me?" requests

✅ What actually works:

1. Search for 2nd degree connections on @linkedin
2. Get introduced through your mutual connection
3. Have a genuine coffee chat about THEIR role
4. Follow up within 24 hours with specific takeaways
5. One week later, show them you acted on their advice

🤯 A real-life example that impressed the hell out of me:

- She attended 5 online workshops we hosted (you can search for Ads on Air, these are all free workshops anyone can attend)

- She messaged every single speaker after the workshop with thoughtful questions and learnings

- Three speakers agreed to a coffee chat and one ended up referring her

The principle: if something is easy for you to do, it is just as easy for everyone else. Easy rarely stands out.

**Why it works:** opens on a hard stat, splits wrong-way / right-way with ❌/✅ so it skims fast, proves it with one concrete story, and closes with a memorable principle.

## Example 2 — Framework Explainer

Most people *save* AI prompts wrong.

Here's why (and how to fix it):

🔴 The Problem: "Found a great prompt! Let me save it in my prompts database."
Result → Never uses it again.

🟡 Why This Happens: Out of sight = out of mind.
Your workflow doesn't care about your folder structure. The best prompt is useless if you forget it exists.

🟢 The Solution: Organize by where you'll USE it, not where you FOUND it.

Example:

Within my weekly 1:1 notes document, I have a link to a prompt that converts my rough thoughts into a structured agenda.

As you can see, the key is to embed these AI touchpoints directly where you're already working.

Stop collecting prompts.
Start embedding them.

#ai #productivity

**Why it works:** a "you're doing it wrong" hook, a red / yellow / green problem-cause-fix structure that's easy to follow, one concrete personal example, and a crisp "stop X, start Y" close.

## Example 3 — Quick Tactical Tip

I used to give #ChatGPT super awkward instructions.

"Make this sound a bit better, like, you know, just flow smoother."

I can't be the only one right? 😅

After lots of trial and error, I've found a much cleaner way to communicate with AI.

Here are my 3 favorite one-word commands that get better results:

1️⃣ ELABORATE - When you need more detail

E.g. "Elaborate on these 3 bullet points."

2️⃣ CRITIQUE - When you want to spot problems early

E.g. "I'm arguing for more headcount based on this data, critique my approach before my presentation."

3️⃣ REWRITE - When you need to improve existing content

E.g. "Rewrite the second paragraph using a friendly tone of voice."

Simple commands = clearer communication = better results.

What's your go-to ChatGPT command? Drop it in the comments below.

#ai

**Why it works:** a self-deprecating, relatable hook with an "I can't be the only one" softener, a clean numbered emoji list of three commands with examples, and an engagement question to drive comments.
