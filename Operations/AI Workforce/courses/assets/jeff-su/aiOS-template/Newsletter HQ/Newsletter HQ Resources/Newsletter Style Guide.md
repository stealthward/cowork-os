# Newsletter Style Guide

_This file contains the structure and voice rules for your newsletter editions. Read it at session start whenever you draft or review any edition. It does NOT contain subject-line strategy (that lives in `Newsletter HQ/Newsletter HQ Resources/Subject Line Strategy.md`) or your universal voice rules (those live in `00_Reference/Voice Principles.md`, which this guide layers on top of)._

## Edition Structure (Native Edition)

A native edition is one tip, explored deeply: one friction point, one fix, with enough context and explanation to make it land. The skeleton is the same one you reuse every time: subject line, opening, the tip, an optional bonus, and the closing.

**Position the tip as evergreen, never as a feature announcement.** Body copy never signals newness ("we can now," "just added," "new feature," or anything that dates the edition). Write as if the capability has always existed, so the edition reads the same a year later. Open with the friction (a question or a second-person scenario the reader recognizes), introduce the fix as the resolution of that friction ("So wouldn't it be great if you could..."), then go straight to the steps and your real example.

### 1. Subject Line

Format: `[Topic]: [Hook]`, 40 to 60 characters, never over 60. Full strategy lives in `Newsletter HQ/Newsletter HQ Resources/Subject Line Strategy.md`.

### 2. Opening Line

Keep it brief and warm, for example "Hey friends -". No welcome ceremony.

### 3. Context Sentence

One sentence that sets up what the edition covers, flowing into a short transition ("so let's dive right in! 😁") as the tail of the same sentence. The transition is the end of the thought, not its own beat. The context sentence should add new information (what the reader will get, why this tip), not restate the subject-line promise. The reader already committed based on the subject line; the opening earns that commitment by reframing, not re-selling.

### 4. The Tip

**Format:**

```
[Descriptive header for the tip]

[Relatable friction point, often with humor or personality in a parenthetical.]

Here's the faster way:

1. [Step 1]
2. [Step 2]
3. [Step 3]

[Screenshot or GIF placeholder]

💡 [Pro tip or a couple of things to know]
```

**Structure rules:**

- Use a descriptive header for the tip. Drop any "Tip #1:" numbering on a single-tip edition; numbering implies more tips are coming.
- Lead with the friction (the problem this solves), then break to a new paragraph for the fix introduction ("Here's the faster way:"). The paragraph break gives the reader a beat to feel the pain before the solution.
- Keep steps to 3 to 5. Each step is a short imperative (Press, Click, Select).
- Include `[screenshot: description]` for static UI, or `[gif: description]` for state changes, after the steps.
- Add a 💡 callout with a pro tip or edge case only when it is genuinely useful. The test: if removing it does not hurt the reader's ability to use the tip, cut it. A pro tip must deepen the current tip, not branch to an adjacent tool.
- **Verify steps against the live product.** Menus and labels change. Test the feature, or match your own screenshots exactly when you have them, before finalizing step text. Never invent a step the screenshots do not show.
- **Disclose any paid requirement up front.** When a tip needs a paid plan, add a one-line "Heads up:" right after the opening, not buried at the end. Drop exact prices (they go stale). When there is a free workaround, give it its own `### Free workaround` header so it is scannable.

### 5. Bonus Section

An optional short bonus, scoped to the edition's topic (for example, a handful of relevant keyboard shortcuts). Scale it to the edition: keep it from outweighing the main tip. Cross-reference your edition log so you do not repeat the same bonus from a recent edition.

### 6. Closing

Formula: a specific action for this edition + "and let me know how it goes! 😁". Default: "Try this out this week and let me know how it goes! 😁". Adapt the action clause when the edition's ask is more specific; the "let me know how it goes! 😁" tail does not change.

## Voice Rules (Newsletter-Specific)

These layer on top of `00_Reference/Voice Principles.md`. Voice Principles owns all the universal rules (personality devices, the phrase blacklist, the em dash ban, motivational-phrasing restrictions). This section owns only the newsletter-specific layering.

### Brevity & Editing

- Shorter than a video script or a long post. Every sentence must earn its place.
- Expect to trim most of a first draft to newsletter length.
- If a line does not move the reader closer to trying the tip, cut it.
- One pass for clarity, one pass for cuts, done.

### Personality Devices

Voice Principles owns the full rules for parentheticals, strikethrough, reader callouts, and in-group identity. Newsletter-specific application note: aim for 2 to 4 parentheticals per edition, each with edge and specificity.

### Emoji Usage (Newsletter-Specific)

Use sparingly and deliberately. Total per edition: 2 to 4. No more.

- 😁, closings and moments of lightness (2 to 3 per edition)
- 😩, a frustration beat (1 per edition if it fits)
- 😛, a self-deprecating moment (1 per edition if it fits)
- 💡, pro-tip callouts (1 to 2 per edition)

### Formatting Rules

| Element | Format |
|---|---|
| Tip header | Bold, descriptive. Drop numbering on a single-tip edition. |
| UI element names | **Bold** (buttons, menus, fields) |
| Keyboard shortcuts | Spelled out, with both Mac and Windows versions where relevant |
| Screenshots | `[screenshot: description]` (bracketed placeholder) |
| GIFs | `[gif: description]` (bracketed placeholder) |
| Pro tips | 💡 callout line |
| Emojis | 😁 😩 😛 💡 only |

## Pre-Publish Checklist

**Structure:**

- [ ] Subject line has a topic prefix plus a hook (40 to 60 characters)
- [ ] Opening is brief and warm
- [ ] Context sentence ends with a short transition ("so let's dive right in! 😁")
- [ ] The tip has a descriptive header, the friction-first lead, numbered steps (3 to 5), a screenshot or GIF placeholder, and a 💡 pro tip when useful
- [ ] Single-tip editions drop "Tip #1:" numbering
- [ ] Paid-plan tips have a "Heads up:" disclosure plus a `### Free workaround` section
- [ ] Closing follows the formula: edition-specific action + "and let me know how it goes! 😁"

**Voice (full rules in `00_Reference/Voice Principles.md`):**

- [ ] Voice Principles pass (no em dashes, no banned phrases, no motivational phrasing)
- [ ] Parentheticals have edge and specificity (2 to 4 per edition)
- [ ] Emoji count is 2 to 4 total
