# Interview Prep: The CARL Framework

_Use this reference to structure concise behavioral-interview answers that show both results and reflection._

## The Framework

### Context

Set up the situation and what you were trying to accomplish. Keep this short so the answer reaches your contribution quickly.

### Action

Explain what you personally did. Focus on the decisions, tradeoffs, and actions that mattered most.

### Results

Describe what changed. Use a measurable outcome when one is available, but do not invent a metric simply to make the story sound stronger.

### Learnings

Close with what the experience taught you and how you would apply that lesson in a similar situation. This is the part that shows reflection and growth.

## Quick Answer Template

- **Context:** What was happening, and what did you need to accomplish?
- **Action:** What did you do, and why?
- **Results:** What changed because of your actions?
- **Learnings:** What did you learn, and what would you carry forward?

## Example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you went above and beyond at work.

- **Context:** I had downtime between consulting projects and saw that new hires lacked a practical onboarding resource.
- **Action:** I designed a training program using the questions and mistakes I had seen most often.
- **Results:** New hires had a clearer starting point and needed less repeated support from experienced team members.
- **Learnings:** Useful internal resources start with a recurring user problem, not with the amount of information available.

## Using CARL With Cowork

Give Cowork the job description, your resume or experience bank, and this framework. Ask it to infer likely behavioral questions from the role and draft CARL outlines using only supported facts. Review the outline and add any missing details before practicing the spoken answer.
